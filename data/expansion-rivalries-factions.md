## DATASET C — MORE Iconic Rivalries & Storylines
===================================================================================

### C1. The Rock vs. Triple H
- **slug:** rock-vs-triple-h
- **Parties:** The Rock vs. Triple H (with DX/Corporation proxies, Chyna, Mankind orbiting)
- **Promotion / era:** WWF, 1998–2000
- **What it was about:** Two rising Attitude Era stars who climbed the card in parallel — Rock through the Nation of Domination and Corporation, Triple H through D-Generation X. Their rivalry was the engine of the mid-to-upper card before both ascended to the very top, trading the Intercontinental and later WWF Championships and headlining constantly.
- **Key matches:** Ladder match for the Intercontinental Title, SummerSlam 1998; the WWF Title Iron Man match on the first-ever prime-time Raw ("Raw Is War" special, 2000) where Rock beat Triple H; countless Raw/PPV singles bouts through 1999–2000.
- **Payoff:** They effectively passed the torch to each other repeatedly, and their chemistry proved both men could carry the company — Rock as the electrifying showman, Triple H as "The Game" workhorse heel. It set up Triple H's dominant 2000 run and Rock's movie-bound megastardom.
- **Graph links:** Rock ↔ Triple H were the two young pillars after Austin; both feuded with Mankind (Foley); Triple H's DX vs. Rock's Nation crosses into dx-vs-nation.

### C2. Mankind / Mick Foley vs. The Rock (Rock 'n' Sock & the "I Quit" war)
- **slug:** mankind-vs-rock
- **Parties:** Mankind (Mick Foley) vs. The Rock
- **Promotion / era:** WWF, 1998–1999
- **What it was about:** A brutal-then-hilarious dual dynamic. As enemies they staged some of the era's most violent matches over the WWF Title; as friends they formed the beloved comedy tag team the "Rock 'n' Sock Connection," whose "This Is Your Life" segment drew one of the highest quarter-hour ratings in Raw history.
- **Key matches:** The Royal Rumble 1999 "I Quit" match, where Rock handcuffed Foley and hit him with roughly a dozen unprotected chair shots (documented in the film "Beyond the Mat"); the Empty Arena match on the post-Super Bowl Halftime Heat 1999.
- **Payoff:** The feud made Foley a top-level main-eventer despite his unconventional look and cemented Rock's mic supremacy. Their friendship/rivalry duality became a template for face/heel storytelling.
- **Graph links:** Foley ↔ Rock; Foley also central to undertaker-vs-mankind (below); Rock ↔ Triple H, Austin.

### C3. The Undertaker vs. Mankind (Hell in a Cell)
- **slug:** undertaker-vs-mankind
- **Parties:** The Undertaker vs. Mankind (Mick Foley)
- **Promotion / era:** WWF, 1996–1998
- **What it was about:** A physically punishing rivalry between the supernatural Deadman and the masochistic Mankind that peaked in the most infamous match in company history.
- **Key matches:** Hell in a Cell at King of the Ring 1998 — Undertaker threw Foley off the top of the 16-foot cell through the announce table, then chokeslammed him through the cell roof onto the ring; Boiler Room Brawl, SummerSlam 1996; Buried Alive match, 1996.
- **Payoff:** The King of the Ring 1998 bumps became the defining image of Attitude Era risk and turned Foley into a folk hero. It deepened both men's legacies and set up Foley's eventual WWF Title win later in 1998.
- **Graph links:** Foley ↔ Undertaker; Undertaker later reconciled/feuded with Kane (undertaker-vs-kane); Foley ↔ Rock.

### C4. John Cena vs. Edge
- **slug:** cena-vs-edge
- **Parties:** John Cena vs. Edge (with Lita)
- **Promotion / era:** WWE, 2006
- **What it was about:** Edge cashed in the first-ever Money in the Bank to dethrone Cena and became the smarmy "Rated-R Superstar," positioning himself as the anti-Cena the vocal adult crowd craved. It crystallized the "Let's Go Cena / Cena Sucks" split audience that defined Cena's whole career.
- **Key matches:** Edge cashing in MITB to beat Cena, New Year's Revolution 2006; Cena regaining the title, Royal Rumble 2006; the TLC match on Raw; and the Unforgiven 2006 street fight in Edge's hometown of Toronto.
- **Payoff:** The rivalry established Edge as a legitimate top-tier heel main-eventer and gave Cena a defining foil, feeding the crowd-polarization that made Cena the biggest star of the PG era.
- **Graph links:** Cena ↔ Edge; Edge ↔ Lita ↔ Matt Hardy (edge-lita-matt); Cena ↔ Orton (below), Lesnar (below), AJ Styles (below).

### C5. John Cena vs. Randy Orton
- **slug:** cena-vs-orton
- **Parties:** John Cena vs. Randy Orton (Orton with Legacy)
- **Promotion / era:** WWE, 2007–2009 (recurring through 2013–2014)
- **What it was about:** The two faces of the post-Attitude generation — the never-quit hero Cena versus the cold, calculating "Legend Killer"/"Viper" Orton — traded the WWE Championship for years. Orton's Legacy stable (with Ted DiBiase Jr. and Cody Rhodes) attacked Cena's family in storyline, escalating the personal stakes.
- **Key matches:** SummerSlam 2007; an Iron Man match on Raw (2009); their WrestleMania XXV feud thread; Cena vs. Orton at Bragging Rights 2009 (Anything Goes) and the TLC match at Breaking Point/2013's TLC unifying the titles.
- **Payoff:** Their rivalry was WWE's most-booked main-event program of the late-2000s and unified the WWE and World Heavyweight Championships at TLC 2013. It anchored a full era.
- **Graph links:** Cena ↔ Orton; Orton ↔ Legacy (Cody Rhodes, Ted DiBiase Jr.); Orton ↔ Christian (below); Cena ↔ Edge.

### C6. Randy Orton vs. Christian
- **slug:** orton-vs-christian
- **Parties:** Randy Orton vs. Christian
- **Promotion / era:** WWE (SmackDown), 2011
- **What it was about:** After Edge's sudden retirement, longtime "second banana" Christian finally won the World Heavyweight Title — only to lose it to Orton days later, sparking a resentful, character-rich feud where Christian's bitter "one more match" entitlement made him a superb heel.
- **Key matches:** Christian def. Alberto Del Rio to win the vacant title (Extreme Rules 2011); Orton def. Christian, Capitol Punishment 2011; their Money in the Bank 2011 cage-adjacent classic and the No Holds Barred match at SummerSlam 2011.
- **Payoff:** It gave Christian the credible main-event singles run he'd been denied for a decade and produced some of 2011's best-worked matches, showcasing Orton as a top babyface.
- **Graph links:** Christian ↔ Edge (lifelong real friends/tag partners, from tlc-tag-wars); Orton ↔ Cena; Christian ↔ Randy Orton.

### C7. Shawn Michaels vs. Chris Jericho
- **slug:** hbk-vs-jericho
- **Parties:** Shawn Michaels vs. Chris Jericho
- **Promotion / era:** WWE, 2008–2009
- **What it was about:** A masterclass in slow-burn character work. Jericho, jealous of the adoration HBK still received, turned heel by accidentally-then-deliberately punching Michaels through the Jeritron screen, reinventing himself as a suit-wearing, cerebral "honest man." He attacked Michaels' wife at ringside, making it deeply personal.
- **Key matches:** Ladder match for the World Heavyweight Title, No Mercy 2008 (widely rated among the best matches of its decade); their Unforgiven 2008 bout; Judgment Day 2008.
- **Payoff:** The feud completely rehabilitated Jericho as a top heel and gave Michaels one of the finest rivalries of his late career. The No Mercy ladder match is regarded as a modern classic.
- **Graph links:** HBK ↔ Jericho; HBK part of DX (below) and the Kliq; Jericho ↔ Kenny Omega (below), AEW founder-era.

### C8. CM Punk vs. Jeff Hardy
- **slug:** punk-vs-jeff-hardy
- **Parties:** CM Punk vs. Jeff Hardy
- **Promotion / era:** WWE (SmackDown), 2009
- **What it was about:** The clearest "virtue vs. vice" morality play of its era — Punk's self-righteous "straight edge" heel (who cashed in Money in the Bank on Hardy) preached sobriety and moral superiority against Hardy's reckless, high-flying daredevil beloved by the crowd, with pointed real-life echoes of Hardy's substance struggles.
- **Key matches:** Punk cashing in MITB on Hardy (Night of Champions 2009); their SummerSlam 2009 TLC match; the SmackDown steel cage "loser leaves WWE" match Hardy lost, writing out his exit.
- **Payoff:** It elevated Punk's heel persona toward his later Straight Edge Society and eventual main-event run, and served as the send-off to Hardy's WWE tenure before his TNA jump.
- **Graph links:** Punk ↔ Jeff Hardy; Punk ↔ Cena (cm-punk-pipebomb); Jeff Hardy ↔ Matt Hardy (brothers, from tlc-tag-wars); Punk indie roots ↔ Colt Cabana, Samoa Joe.

### C9. AJ Styles vs. John Cena
- **slug:** styles-vs-cena
- **Parties:** AJ Styles vs. John Cena
- **Promotion / era:** WWE, 2016
- **What it was about:** A genuine dream match. Styles, a decorated TNA/NJPW icon long deemed "too indie" for WWE, arrived in 2016 and immediately proved he belonged by going toe-to-toe with the company's franchise player, symbolizing the validation of the wider wrestling world inside WWE.
- **Key matches:** Cena def. Styles in an instant classic at SummerSlam 2016; Styles def. Cena via Money in the Bank cash-in threads and their SmackDown series; Styles beat Cena for the WWE Title at Royal Rumble 2017 as Cena chased his 16th reign.
- **Payoff:** It legitimized AJ Styles as a top WWE star ("phenomenal" run to the WWE Championship) and gave Cena one of the best pure-wrestling rivalries of his later career.
- **Graph links:** Styles ↔ Cena; Styles ↔ Samoa Joe & Christopher Daniels (TNA, styles-vs-daniels-vs-joe); Styles ↔ Nakamura (below); Styles ↔ Bullet Club (below).

### C10. AJ Styles vs. Shinsuke Nakamura
- **slug:** styles-vs-nakamura
- **Parties:** AJ Styles vs. Shinsuke Nakamura
- **Promotion / era:** NJPW (2016) and WWE (2018)
- **What it was about:** A cross-promotional dream match. The two headlined Wrestle Kingdom 10 in New Japan (Styles' final NJPW match), then reunited in WWE two years later after Nakamura won the 2018 Royal Rumble to challenge Styles for the WWE Title at WrestleMania 34.
- **Key matches:** Nakamura vs. Styles, Wrestle Kingdom 10 (2016, Tokyo Dome); Styles vs. Nakamura, WrestleMania 34 (2018), which ended with Nakamura's post-match low-blow heel turn; their string of increasingly violent SmackDown rematches (Last Man Standing, etc.) through 2018.
- **Payoff:** The WrestleMania match underwhelmed relative to sky-high expectations, but the low-blow turn rebuilt Nakamura as a credible heel and their feud produced strong follow-ups. It stands as a marquee example of NJPW talent reshaping WWE's main event.
- **Graph links:** Styles ↔ Nakamura (both ex-NJPW/Bullet Club-adjacent); Styles ↔ Cena; Nakamura later ↔ Sami Zayn (NXT), King of Strong Style lineage.

### C11. Sami Zayn vs. Kevin Owens
- **slug:** sami-vs-owens
- **Parties:** Sami Zayn (El Generico) vs. Kevin Owens (Kevin Steen)
- **Promotion / era:** Indies (ROH/PWG) → WWE NXT (2014) → WWE main roster (2015–2023)
- **What it was about:** The rivalry rooted in the most real friendship in wrestling. Best friends since the Quebec indie scene, they cycle endlessly between allies and enemies. Owens debuted in NXT by powerbombing Zayn on the apron moments after Zayn won the NXT Title, then betrayed him again after seemingly reforming during the Bloodline saga.
- **Key matches:** Owens def. Zayn for the NXT Championship (TakeOver: R Evolution 2014); their NXT rematch; Battleground 2015; and the emotional coda where they teamed to beat The Usos at WrestleMania 39 (2023), only for tension to resurface.
- **Payoff:** Their decades-spanning bond gave WWE a renewable, authentic story engine. The 2023 WrestleMania tag win (with Owens saving Zayn from the Bloodline) was one of the most cathartic moments of that era.
- **Graph links:** Zayn ↔ Owens (real best friends); Zayn ↔ The Bloodline (honorary "Uce," the-bloodline); both ↔ NXT golden era; Zayn ↔ Nakamura.

### C12. Gunther vs. Sheamus
- **slug:** gunther-vs-sheamus
- **Parties:** Gunther vs. Sheamus
- **Promotion / era:** WWE, 2022–2023
- **What it was about:** A hard-hitting, old-school battle of pride over the Intercontinental Championship between two of the stiffest strikers in the company — the Austrian "Ring General" Gunther defending his record-setting reign against the grizzled Irish brawler Sheamus, both fighting for the prestige of the title itself.
- **Key matches:** Gunther def. Sheamus, Clash at the Castle 2022 (Cardiff) — an instant match-of-the-year contender; the WrestleMania 39 (2023) triple threat with Drew McIntyre added, another widely acclaimed bout.
- **Payoff:** The Clash at the Castle war restored the Intercontinental Championship's importance and cemented Gunther's dominant reign (the longest IC reign in history) while proving Sheamus a beloved elder statesman.
- **Graph links:** Gunther ↔ Sheamus; Gunther ↔ Imperium (below); Gunther later ↔ world title run vs. Cody Rhodes; Sheamus ↔ Cesaro (The Bar).

### C13. Cody Rhodes vs. Roman Reigns
- **slug:** cody-vs-roman
- **Parties:** Cody Rhodes vs. Roman Reigns (Tribal Chief, with The Bloodline)
- **Promotion / era:** WWE, 2023–2024
- **What it was about:** The climactic chapter of the Bloodline saga framed as Cody's quest to "finish the story" — to win the world title his late father Dusty Rhodes never did — against Reigns' record-length Undisputed reign backed by his family faction.
- **Key matches:** Reigns def. Cody, WrestleMania 39 (2023, a controversial ending); Cody def. Reigns, WrestleMania XL Night 2 (2024) in a "Bloodline Rules" match with cameo run-ins from Seth Rollins, John Cena, Undertaker, and a face-turned Usos, ending Reigns' ~1,316-day reign.
- **Payoff:** The delayed, one-year-later payoff produced one of the most cathartic WrestleMania moments ever and coronated Cody as the new franchise face of WWE. (Distinct here from the-bloodline entry, which covers the full multi-year family arc.)
- **Graph links:** Cody ↔ Roman; Cody ↔ Dusty & Dustin Rhodes (Rhodes family); Cody ↔ AEW co-founder past; Roman ↔ The Bloodline (Usos, Solo Sikoa, Heyman, Sami Zayn).

### C14. Rhea Ripley vs. Charlotte Flair
- **slug:** rhea-vs-charlotte
- **Parties:** Rhea Ripley vs. Charlotte Flair
- **Promotion / era:** WWE NXT (2020) and main roster (2023)
- **What it was about:** A generational women's rivalry — the dominant, edgy Australian Ripley challenging the pedigreed second-generation queen Charlotte for supremacy. Charlotte even dropped down to NXT to challenge the young champion, an unusual "veteran invades the developmental brand" dynamic.
- **Key matches:** Charlotte def. Ripley for the NXT Women's Title, WrestleMania 36 (2020, the empty-"Performance Center" pandemic show); Ripley def. Charlotte to retain the SmackDown Women's Title, WrestleMania 39 (2023), a highly praised main-card bout.
- **Payoff:** The WrestleMania 39 win was Ripley's arrival as WWE's premier women's star and the centerpiece of Judgment Day, while affirming Charlotte's role as the measuring stick for every top woman of the era.
- **Graph links:** Rhea ↔ Charlotte; Rhea ↔ Judgment Day (below), Dominik Mysterio ("Mami"); Charlotte ↔ Ric Flair (father), the Four Horsewomen (below); Charlotte ↔ Becky Lynch, Sasha Banks, Bayley.

### C15. Becky Lynch "The Man" Rise (vs. Charlotte Flair & Ronda Rousey)
- **slug:** becky-the-man
- **Parties:** Becky Lynch vs. Charlotte Flair vs. Ronda Rousey
- **Promotion / era:** WWE, 2018–2019
- **What it was about:** A grassroots babyface explosion. When Becky turned on her friend Charlotte at SummerSlam 2018, fans overwhelmingly cheered the "heel," and WWE pivoted her into the anti-authority "The Man." Her feud with UFC-crossover star Ronda Rousey built to the first women's match to main-event WrestleMania.
- **Key matches:** Becky vs. Charlotte, SmackDown/Evolution 2018; the Winner-Take-All triple threat vs. Charlotte Flair and Ronda Rousey at WrestleMania 35 (2019), where Becky pinned Rousey to hold both women's titles — the first women's WrestleMania main event.
- **Payoff:** Becky became the biggest women's star of the era and the face of WWE's "women's evolution," a fan-powered coronation comparable to Daniel Bryan's Yes Movement.
- **Graph links:** Becky ↔ Charlotte ↔ Bayley ↔ Sasha Banks (the Four Horsewomen, below); Becky ↔ Ronda Rousey; Becky ↔ Seth Rollins (married in real life).

### C16. Brock Lesnar vs. John Cena ("Suplex City")
- **slug:** lesnar-vs-cena
- **Parties:** Brock Lesnar (with Paul Heyman) vs. John Cena
- **Promotion / era:** WWE, 2012 and 2014–2015
- **What it was about:** The returning UFC-conqueror "Beast" Lesnar positioned as a legitimate destroyer against WWE's franchise face. Their 2014 chapter turned Cena's Superman aura on its head.
- **Key matches:** Lesnar def. Cena to win the WWE World Heavyweight Title at SummerSlam 2014 in a near-total squash (16 German suplexes, "Suplex City"), one of the most shocking one-sided main events ever; their Night of Champions 2014 rematch; Extreme Rules 2012.
- **Payoff:** The SummerSlam 2014 demolition rebuilt Lesnar as an unstoppable monster (setting up his eventual Undertaker streak-ending aura and Reigns feuds) and showed WWE willing to make even Cena look mortal.
- **Graph links:** Lesnar ↔ Cena; Lesnar ↔ Paul Heyman (advocate, later Reigns' Wise Man); Lesnar ↔ Kurt Angle (2003), Undertaker, Roman Reigns.

### C17. Sting vs. Jeff Jarrett
- **slug:** sting-vs-jeff-jarrett
- **Parties:** Sting vs. Jeff Jarrett
- **Promotion / era:** WCW (2000) and TNA (2005–2006, recurring)
- **What it was about:** Jarrett cast himself as the self-proclaimed "King of the Mountain" and franchise owner-figure, first in the dying days of WCW and then as the founder/top heel of TNA, with Sting as the veteran conscience repeatedly brought in to dethrone him.
- **Key matches:** Their WCW 2000 title programs (in the Russo-booked "New Blood" era); Sting def. Jarrett for the NWA World Title at TNA Bound for Glory 2006 (with Kurt Angle as special enforcer).
- **Payoff:** The Bound for Glory 2006 win gave TNA a signature "old guard hero triumphs" moment and helped legitimize the company's flagship event, with Sting as its elder statesman.
- **Graph links:** Sting ↔ Jarrett; Jarrett founded TNA (later co-founded Global Force / GFW); Sting ↔ nWo/Hogan (sting-vs-flair, nwo-invasion); Jarrett ↔ Fortune/Immortal & Aces angles.

### C18. Kurt Angle vs. Samoa Joe (TNA)
- **slug:** angle-vs-joe
- **Parties:** Kurt Angle vs. Samoa Joe
- **Promotion / era:** TNA, 2007–2008
- **What it was about:** TNA's biggest signing (Olympic gold medalist Angle, fresh from WWE) against its homegrown undefeated monster Joe — a clash of pure amateur-mat credibility versus indie-forged toughness, later complicated by the on-screen involvement of Angle's real-life wife Karen.
- **Key matches:** 30-minute Iron Man match, Final Resolution (January 2007); Angle def. Joe, Hard Justice 2007; their series across Genesis and Sacrifice 2007.
- **Payoff:** The rivalry is often called TNA's greatest feud; it gave the promotion two credible main-event pillars and validated its "sports-based" alternative identity, though Joe's momentum was later blunted.
- **Graph links:** Angle ↔ Joe; Joe ↔ AJ Styles & Christopher Daniels (styles-vs-daniels-vs-joe, and X-Division); Angle ↔ Sting, Jeff Jarrett (TNA); Angle ↔ Brock Lesnar, Benoit (WWE past).

### C19. Chris Jericho vs. Kenny Omega ("Alpha vs. Omega")
- **slug:** jericho-vs-omega
- **Parties:** Chris Jericho vs. Kenny Omega
- **Promotion / era:** NJPW (2018) and AEW (2019–2020)
- **What it was about:** A cross-generational, cross-promotional dream feud that began when veteran Jericho shockingly attacked young NJPW ace Omega, then carried over as a foundational rivalry of the brand-new AEW, where both were pillars.
- **Key matches:** Omega def. Jericho, Wrestle Kingdom 12 (2018, Tokyo Dome); Jericho def. Omega at AEW's debut PPV Double or Nothing 2019 to become AEW's first marquee star; their "Mimosa Mayhem" and follow-ups.
- **Payoff:** The Wrestle Kingdom bout is credited with drawing enormous Western attention to NJPW and helping catalyze the AEW founding; Jericho's Double or Nothing win made him AEW's first world champion shortly after.
- **Graph links:** Jericho ↔ Omega; Omega ↔ The Young Bucks & Cody Rhodes (AEW founders / The Elite); Jericho ↔ HBK; Omega ↔ Okada (below).

### C20. Kazuchika Okada vs. Kenny Omega (NJPW)
- **slug:** okada-vs-omega
- **Parties:** Kazuchika Okada vs. Kenny Omega
- **Promotion / era:** NJPW, 2017–2018
- **What it was about:** The defining pure-wrestling rivalry of the 2010s — NJPW's ace "Rainmaker" Okada against the Canadian "Cleaner" Omega, leader of Bullet Club — a series of epic, long-form IWGP Heavyweight Championship matches that drew unprecedented international acclaim.
- **Key matches:** Their four-match saga: Wrestle Kingdom 11 (Jan 2017, the famous near-6-star bout), a 60-minute draw at Dominion (June 2017), G1 Climax (Aug 2017), and Omega finally defeating Okada in a 2-out-of-3 falls match at Dominion (June 2018) to win the IWGP title.
- **Payoff:** The series is widely regarded as some of the greatest professional wrestling ever staged, drawing legions of new Western fans to NJPW and elevating both men to global icon status.
- **Graph links:** Okada ↔ Omega; Omega ↔ Bullet Club, The Elite, Jericho; Okada ↔ Tanahashi, Naito (Los Ingobernables, below); the rivalry directly fed AEW's launch.

### C21. Bret Hart vs. Steve Austin (the Double Turn)
- **slug:** austin-vs-bret
- **Parties:** Bret "Hitman" Hart vs. "Stone Cold" Steve Austin
- **Promotion / era:** WWF, 1996–1997
- **What it was about:** A generational clash between traditional-hero Bret and the rising anti-hero Austin. Their feud produced pro wrestling's textbook "double turn," fueling Bret's anti-American heel run and Austin's ascent as the ultimate babyface rebel.
- **Key matches:** The submission match at WrestleMania 13 (1997) — Austin, trapped in the Sharpshooter, refused to quit and passed out in a pool of his own blood, turning face while Bret turned heel; their earlier Survivor Series 1996 opener.
- **Payoff:** The double turn is taught as the gold standard of the technique; it launched Austin's rocket to superstardom and set up Bret's bitter path toward the Montreal Screwjob months later (bret-vs-hbk-montreal).
- **Graph links:** Austin ↔ Bret; Bret ↔ HBK, Vince McMahon (Montreal); Austin ↔ Rock, McMahon; Bret ↔ Hart family, Owen Hart, Davey Boy Smith.

### D1. D-Generation X (DX)
- **slug:** d-generation-x
- **Members:** Founders Shawn Michaels & Triple H with Chyna and Rick Rude (1997); later "New DX" — Triple H, X-Pac (Sean Waltman), Road Dogg, Billy Gunn (New Age Outlaws), Chyna (1998–99); revived 2006 as Triple H & Shawn Michaels.
- **Promotion / era:** WWF/WWE, 1997–2000, revivals 2006 and 2009.
- **Significance:** The rebellious, crude, "suck it" anti-establishment crew that embodied the Attitude Era's edginess and helped WWF win the Monday Night War. Launched or elevated the careers of Triple H, X-Pac, and the New Age Outlaws.
- **Notable feuds:** DX vs. The Nation of Domination (dx-vs-nation); DX vs. The Corporation/Mr. McMahon; the "invasion" of WCW's Nitro tapings in a Jeep; DX vs. Rated-RKO (2006).
- **Graph links:** Overlaps The Kliq (Michaels, Triple H, X-Pac ↔ Nash & Hall in WCW); Triple H → Evolution (below); HBK → hbk-vs-jericho.

### D2. nWo Wolfpac (and the nWo splinters)
- **slug:** nwo-wolfpac
- **Members:** nWo Wolfpac (red-and-black, 1998) — Kevin Nash, "Macho Man" Randy Savage, Konnan, Lex Luger, Sting, later Rey Mysterio; opposed the original nWo Hollywood (white-and-black) — Hollywood Hogan, Scott Hall, Scott Steiner, The Giant, Bret Hart, Buff Bagwell.
- **Promotion / era:** WCW, 1998–1999.
- **Significance:** When the New World Order grew too large, WCW split it into two warring factions — the babyface-leaning Wolfpac and heel nWo Hollywood — briefly reinvigorating the angle. It also demonstrated the bloat and lack of direction that ultimately killed the nWo concept and, with it, much of WCW's momentum.
- **Notable feuds:** nWo Wolfpac vs. nWo Hollywood; both nWo factions vs. WCW loyalists (Goldberg, DDP); the endless internal power struggles.
- **Graph links:** Extends nwo-invasion & sting-vs-nwo (base file); Nash & Hall ↔ The Kliq/DX; Sting crossed from Wolfpac; Hogan ↔ Sting (Starrcade). Later the nWo reformed briefly in WWE (2002).

### D3. Evolution
- **slug:** evolution
- **Members:** Triple H, Ric Flair, Randy Orton, Batista (Dave Bautista).
- **Promotion / era:** WWE (Raw), 2003–2005; reunion 2014.
- **Significance:** A "past, present, and future" stable built around Triple H, using 16-time champion Flair to legitimize rising stars Orton and Batista. It dominated Raw's main event and served as a star-making machine — Orton became the youngest World Heavyweight Champion and Batista a top face after both eventually split from and feuded with Triple H.
- **Notable feuds:** Evolution vs. The Rock, Mick Foley, Shawn Michaels (2003–04); internal breakup — Orton's ouster after winning the title (2004) and Batista's face turn (2005); Evolution vs. The Shield (2014, see shield-rise-and-betrayal).
- **Graph links:** Triple H ↔ DX, The Authority; Flair ↔ Four Horsemen legacy, Charlotte (daughter); Orton → Legacy (below), cena-vs-orton; Batista ↔ Triple H (WrestleMania 21), Bryan (yes-movement).

### D4. The Nation of Domination
- **slug:** the-nation-of-domination
- **Members:** Led by Faarooq (Ron Simmons); later fronted by The Rock; included D'Lo Brown, Mark Henry, Kama Mustafa, Owen Hart, and (early) Savio Vega/Crush.
- **Promotion / era:** WWF, 1996–1998.
- **Significance:** A militant, socially-charged Black-power-styled stable whose greatest legacy was serving as the launchpad for The Rock, who ousted Faarooq as leader and rocketed to superstardom. Also elevated Mark Henry and D'Lo Brown.
- **Notable feuds:** The Nation vs. D-Generation X (dx-vs-nation); Rock vs. Faarooq (internal power struggle); Nation vs. Ken Shamrock, Steve Austin.
- **Graph links:** Rock → rock-vs-triple-h, rock-vs-austin, mankind-vs-rock; Faarooq → later The APA with Bradshaw; Owen Hart ↔ Hart family; feud with DX.

### D5. The Ministry of Darkness
- **slug:** the-ministry-of-darkness
- **Members:** Led by The Undertaker; included Paul Bearer, The Acolytes (Faarooq & Bradshaw), Mideon, Viscera, The Brood (Gangrel, Edge, Christian), Mabel; later merged with the Corporation into the "Corporate Ministry" fronted by Shane McMahon and a masked "Higher Power" (revealed as Vince McMahon).
- **Promotion / era:** WWF, 1999.
- **Significance:** The darkest, most occult storyline of the Attitude Era, with Undertaker as a satanic cult leader "sacrificing" victims on a symbol and abducting Stephanie McMahon. It tied the McMahon-Austin power struggle to a supernatural through-line and was an early showcase for the debuting Edge & Christian (via The Brood).
- **Notable feuds:** The Ministry vs. Steve Austin & Vince McMahon (the attempted "sacrifice" of Stephanie); Ministry vs. Big Show.

### D6. Right to Censor (RTC)
