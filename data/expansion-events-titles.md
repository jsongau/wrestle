# Marquee Events & Championship Lineages — Reference Dataset

Original synthesis for the MAT pro-wrestling database. Two new content types built for crawlability and internal linking: **landmark events/PPVs** and **major championship lineages**. Dates and names verified against multiple references; descriptions are written fresh, not copied. Slugs follow the project rule (lowercase; drop periods/apostrophes/quotes/parentheses; spaces → hyphens).

Cross-link only to the 41 canonical wrestlers and 5 promotion pages (`wwe`, `wcw`, `ecw`, `tna`, `nxt`) defined in the build manifest; everyone else stays plain text.

# 1) MARQUEE EVENTS / PPVs

Twenty-six landmark shows across WWE/WWF, WCW, ECW, TNA and NXT, with AEW's "big four" noted at the end.

---

## WrestleMania
- **Slug:** wrestlemania
- **Promotion:** WWF/WWE
- **First year / notable editions:** March 31, 1985 (Madison Square Garden). Landmark editions: WrestleMania III (1987, Pontiac Silverdome, reported 93,000+), X-Seven (2001, Houston — widely called the best ever), XXVIII–40 (stadium era), WrestleMania 40 (2024, Philadelphia).
- **What it is:** WWE's flagship, "The Showcase of the Immortals" — the annual season-ending spectacle held each spring, now a two-night stadium event.
- **Historic moments:** Hulk Hogan bodyslamming André the Giant (III); Randy Savage vs. Ricky Steamboat for the Intercontinental Title (III); the Austin–Bret Hart double-turn (13); the two-night Boneyard/Firefly Fun House cinematic era (36); Cody Rhodes "finishing the story" to dethrone Roman Reigns (40).
- **Why it matters:** The event that turned pro wrestling into mainstream pop spectacle and launched the entire modern PPV/premium-live-event model. Careers are defined by WrestleMania moments; The Undertaker's undefeated "Streak" (21–1) was built and broken here.

## Royal Rumble
- **Slug:** royal-rumble
- **Promotion:** WWF/WWE
- **First year / notable editions:** First held January 24, 1988 (USA Network special, 20 entrants); first as a PPV in 1989 (expanded to 30 entrants). Kicks off WWE's "Road to WrestleMania" each January.
- **What it is:** Built around the Royal Rumble match — an over-the-top-rope battle royal with staggered timed entrances; eliminations require both feet touching the floor.
- **Historic moments:** Since 1993 the winner earns a world-title shot at WrestleMania; Ric Flair winning the vacant WWF Title in 1992; "iron man" runs by the likes of Chris Benoit (2004, from #1) and Rey Mysterio (2006); the first women's Royal Rumble in 2018.
- **Why it matters:** The most-imitated match concept in wrestling. Its winner-goes-to-WrestleMania stakes make it the true start of WWE's biggest storytelling season.

## SummerSlam
- **Slug:** summerslam
- **Promotion:** WWF/WWE
- **First year / notable editions:** August 29, 1988 (Madison Square Garden). Notable: 1992 (Wembley Stadium, ~80,000, British Bulldog vs. Bret Hart for the IC Title); 1994, 2002 (Lesnar vs. The Rock), 2013 (Bryan vs. Cena).
- **What it is:** "The Biggest Party of the Summer" — WWE's second-largest annual event, the summer anchor of the calendar.
- **Historic moments:** Davey Boy Smith beating Bret Hart before a home crowd (1992); The Rock vs. Brock Lesnar (2002); Daniel Bryan winning the WWE Title from John Cena, followed by the first Seth Rollins Money-in-the-Bank cash-in tease (2013).
- **Why it matters:** The clear #2 event on WWE's calendar and often a workrate showcase, historically delivering match-of-the-year contenders in a less bombastic setting than WrestleMania.

## Survivor Series
- **Slug:** survivor-series
- **Promotion:** WWF/WWE
- **First year / notable editions:** November 26, 1987 (Thanksgiving night). One of WWE's "Big Four." Reformatted around WarGames matches beginning in 2022.
- **What it is:** Traditionally built on 4-on-4 (or 5-on-5) elimination tag matches; in the modern era a brand-vs-brand showcase, now headlined by WarGames.
- **Historic moments:** The Montreal Screwjob — Bret Hart "screwed" out of the title by Vince McMahon and Shawn Michaels (1997); the in-ring WWF debut of The Undertaker (1990); Team Austin vs. Team Bischoff and the Rock's first WWF Title win in a one-night tournament (1998).
- **Why it matters:** The oldest surviving WWE PPV concept after WrestleMania, home to the industry's single most infamous real-life controversy and, latterly, the mainstream home of the WarGames match.

## Money in the Bank
- **Slug:** money-in-the-bank
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **First year / notable editions:** Ladder match debuted at WrestleMania 21 (2005); spun off into its own PPV in July 2010.
- **What it is:** A multi-man ladder match in which the winner retrieves a briefcase granting a guaranteed world-title shot, cashable any time within (roughly) a year.
- **Historic moments:** Edge winning the first-ever briefcase and cashing in on a bloodied John Cena (New Year's Revolution 2006); CM Punk's "pipebomb"-era WWE Title win over Cena in Chicago (2011); numerous "cash-in" title switches, including Seth Rollins turning his WrestleMania 31 main event into a title win.
- **Why it matters:** The briefcase reinvented booking logic — introducing a permanent, roster-wide threat of a surprise title change and creating dozens of overnight main-eventers.

## King of the Ring
- **Slug:** king-of-the-ring
- **Promotion:** WWF/WWE
- **First year / notable editions:** Tournament dates to 1985 (non-televised); first PPV in 1993. Revived intermittently as a tournament/PLE, including 2024.
- **What it is:** A single-elimination tournament crowning the "King of the Ring," historically a springboard for rising main-eventers.
- **Historic moments:** "Stone Cold" Steve Austin's "Austin 3:16" promo after winning in 1996 — arguably the launch of the Attitude Era's biggest star; Bret Hart (1991) and Kurt Angle (2000) among prestige winners; Mankind vs. The Undertaker's Hell in a Cell (1998) with the cage-top and through-the-roof bumps.
- **Why it matters:** A star-making machine; the 1996 edition produced the single most influential promo of the modern era and the "3:16" merchandising phenomenon.

## Hell in a Cell
- **Slug:** hell-in-a-cell
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **First year / notable editions:** Match debuted at Badd Blood: In Your House (October 5, 1997); became a themed annual PPV from 2009 to 2022.
- **What it is:** A roofed steel cage enclosing the ring and ringside — a blow-off "structure" match designed to end long, violent feuds.
- **Historic moments:** Shawn Michaels vs. The Undertaker (the first, plus Kane's debut); Mankind's iconic falls off and through the cell vs. Undertaker (King of the Ring 1998); Triple H vs. The Undertaker "End of an Era" (WrestleMania 28).
- **Why it matters:** WWE's most mythologized gimmick match — shorthand for maximum danger and feud-ending drama, and the source of some of the most-replayed bumps in company history.

## Elimination Chamber
- **Slug:** elimination-chamber
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **First year / notable editions:** Match debuted at Survivor Series 2002; became its own annual PPV in 2010 (rebranding the February No Way Out slot).
- **What it is:** Six wrestlers, a domed steel structure with four enclosed "pods"; two start, the rest enter at timed intervals, eliminations by pin or submission.
- **Historic moments:** The inaugural chamber won by Shawn Michaels to capture the World Heavyweight Championship (2002); repeated use as a WrestleMania-season title decider; John Cena, Triple H and Brock Lesnar among multi-time participants.
- **Why it matters:** A signature structure that reliably produces high-stakes, physical multi-man storytelling and helps set the WrestleMania main-event picture.

## Backlash
- **Slug:** backlash
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **First year / notable editions:** First held April 25, 1999; long positioned as the post-WrestleMania blow-off show. Relaunched as an international "premium live event" (e.g., Backlash France 2024).
- **What it is:** A spring PPV that historically paid off (or restarted) feuds coming out of WrestleMania.
- **Historic moments:** The Rock vs. Steve Austin rematches; hot international crowds in the PLE era (Puerto Rico 2023, Lyon 2024) reframing the show as a destination event.
- **Why it matters:** A durable secondary anchor and, more recently, a flagship for WWE's international expansion strategy.

## Extreme Rules
- **Slug:** extreme-rules
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **First year / notable editions:** First held June 7, 2009, as the mainstream successor to the ECW-branded One Night Stand.
- **What it is:** A gimmick-match-themed PPV (street fights, cage, ladder, "extreme rules" stipulations) — WWE's outlet for hardcore-flavored storytelling.
- **Historic moments:** Numerous brutal blow-offs; the "Fight Pit" and cinematic stipulation matches in later years.
- **Why it matters:** Institutionalized the "extreme" match brand inside PG-era WWE, giving feuds a violent finish line without a standalone hardcore promotion.

## Starrcade
- **Slug:** starrcade
- **Promotion:** NWA / WCW
- **First year / notable editions:** November 24, 1983 (Greensboro), conceived by Dusty Rhodes as the NWA's answer to a season-ending "super card." Ran through 2000; WWE later revived the name as a house-show brand.
- **What it is:** WCW's WrestleMania — the promotion's flagship year-end spectacle.
- **Historic moments:** Ric Flair winning the NWA World Title from Harley Race in a steel cage (1983); Sting finally confronting Hollywood Hogan after a year of silent stalking (1997); Goldberg vs. Hogan-era title programs.
- **Why it matters:** The most prestigious event in the NWA/WCW lineage and the historic centerpiece of the promotion's rivalry with WWF.

## Bash at the Beach
- **Slug:** bash-at-the-beach
- **Promotion:** WCW
- **First year / notable editions:** July 1994 through 2000; WCW's marquee summer show.
- **What it is:** A beach-themed summer PPV, WCW's seasonal counterpart to SummerSlam.
- **Historic moments:** Hulk Hogan's heel turn and the formation of the New World Order as the "third man" at the 1996 edition — one of the most consequential angles in wrestling history; the infamous 2000 Hogan/Jeff Jarrett "worked-shoot" and Vince Russo shoot promo.
- **Why it matters:** Birthplace of the nWo, the angle that powered WCW to 83 straight weeks of ratings wins over WWF and reshaped the entire industry.

## Halloween Havoc
- **Slug:** halloween-havoc
- **Promotion:** WCW
- **First year / notable editions:** October 1989 through 2000. WWE later revived the name for NXT specials.
- **What it is:** WCW's Halloween-themed October PPV, often elaborately staged (giant pumpkins, spooky sets).
- **Historic moments:** Rey Mysterio Jr. vs. Eddie Guerrero (title vs. mask, 1997) — a benchmark cruiserweight classic; Hulk Hogan vs. The Ultimate Warrior (1998); Goldberg's WCW Title win over Diamond Dallas Page (1998).
- **Why it matters:** Home to some of WCW's best in-ring work, especially the cruiserweight showcases that distinguished the promotion's product.

## Great American Bash
- **Slug:** great-american-bash
- **Promotion:** NWA / WCW (later WWE)
- **First year / notable editions:** Launched by Dusty Rhodes in 1985 as an NWA supercard/tour; a WCW PPV staple through 2000; revived by WWE (2004–2009) as a SmackDown-branded summer show.
- **What it is:** A patriotic summer event, historically the NWA/WCW's outdoor-stadium-flavored super card.
- **Historic moments:** The bloody, emotionally charged NWA main events of the 1980s; WarGames matches; in the WWE era, the SmackDown-brand title programs.
- **Why it matters:** One of the NWA's foundational supercards and an early template for the "seasonal themed PPV," carried across two different eras and companies.

## Clash of the Champions
- **Slug:** clash-of-the-champions
- **Promotion:** NWA / WCW
- **First year / notable editions:** March 27, 1988 — deliberately counter-programmed free on TBS opposite WrestleMania IV. Ran through 1997.
- **What it is:** A free, network-television super card designed to deliver PPV-quality cards to a mass audience and steal viewers from WWF.
- **Historic moments:** Ric Flair vs. Sting 45-minute time-limit draw (the debut edition) that made Sting a star; numerous title changes and hot TV angles.
- **Why it matters:** A pioneering "free big show" strategy — the ancestor of today's special TV episodes — and the launching pad for Sting as WCW's homegrown franchise babyface.

## ECW November to Remember
- **Slug:** november-to-remember
- **Promotion:** ECW
- **First year / notable editions:** 1993 through 2000; ECW's flagship annual event.
- **What it is:** Extreme Championship Wrestling's biggest show of the year — the promotion's "WrestleMania."
- **Historic moments:** Career-defining ECW World Title programs; brutal hardcore blow-offs and the ambitious multi-man main events that built ECW's cult reputation.
- **Why it matters:** The centerpiece of the promotion that pioneered the hardcore/"extreme" style, garnered a rabid cult following, and served as a talent incubator (Austin, Foley, Guerrero, Malenko, RVD and more) for both WWF and WCW.

## ECW Barely Legal
- **Slug:** barely-legal
- **Promotion:** ECW
- **First year / notable editions:** April 13, 1997 — ECW's first-ever pay-per-view.
- **What it is:** The show where a scrappy regional promotion finally broke onto national PPV, its title a wink at the odds against it happening at all.
- **Historic moments:** Terry Funk winning the ECW World Heavyweight Championship in the main event; the event's very existence validating ECW's national ambitions.
- **Why it matters:** Proof-of-concept that an "outlaw" hardcore promotion could reach PPV, cementing ECW as the influential third major of the late-1990s "war."

## ECW One Night Stand
- **Slug:** ecw-one-night-stand
- **Promotion:** ECW (WWE-produced)
- **First year / notable editions:** June 12, 2005 (reunion show); a 2006 sequel that relaunched ECW as a WWE brand.
- **What it is:** A WWE-produced reunion of the original ECW roster, later a themed "extreme rules" PPV.
- **Historic moments:** The venom-soaked 2005 Hammerstein Ballroom crowd; Rob Van Dam cashing in Money in the Bank to win the WWE Championship in 2006 in an ECW-flavored main event vs. John Cena.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrated the enduring passion of the ECW fan base, directly leading to WWE's ECW brand revival and eventually inspiring the Extreme Rules PPV.

## TNA Bound for Glory
- **Slug:** bound-for-glory
- **Promotion:** TNA
- **First year / notable editions:** October 23, 2005 — TNA's flagship, held every October.
- **What it is:** TNA/Impact Wrestling's biggest annual event, its equivalent of WrestleMania.
- **Historic moments:** Marquee TNA World Title matches; AJ Styles, Kurt Angle, Sting and Samoa Joe headline programs; the "Bound for Glory Series" tournament arcs feeding into the main event.
- **Why it matters:** TNA's premier show and season-ending destination, the stage for the promotion's most ambitious storytelling as it built the primary alternative to WWE in the 2000s.

## TNA Slammiversary
- **Slug:** slammiversary
- **Promotion:** TNA
- **First year / notable editions:** June 19, 2005 — commemorating TNA's June 2002 launch; held each summer.
- **What it is:** TNA's anniversary PPV, celebrating the promotion's founding and often used for major debuts and returns.
- **Historic moments:** Surprise debuts and re-signings that repeatedly re-energized the brand; King of the Mountain matches; major TNA World Title moments.
- **Why it matters:** The company's self-defining "we're still here" show, frequently the launchpad for new eras and a marquee for shock arrivals — including in the post-2024 return to the TNA name.

## TNA Lockdown
- **Slug:** lockdown
- **Promotion:** TNA
- **First year / notable editions:** 2005 through 2014 — famous for a unique concept.
- **What it is:** A PPV where **every single match** took place inside a steel cage (often TNA's trademark "six sides of steel").
- **Historic moments:** The multi-man "Lethal Lockdown" WarGames-style main events; team-vs-team blow-offs; multiple TNA World Title changes.
- **Why it matters:** A distinctive branding hook no other major promotion matched — an entire PPV of cage matches — that gave TNA a clear identity and annual centerpiece.

## NXT TakeOver
- **Slug:** nxt-takeover
- **Promotion:** NXT (WWE)
- **First year / notable editions:** First held May 29, 2014; the series ran through NXT TakeOver 36 in 2021 before the brand shifted to the "NXT" PLE naming.
- **What it is:** NXT's premium special-event brand — critically acclaimed, often overshadowing WWE's main-roster PPVs on the same weekends.
- **Historic moments:** Sami Zayn vs. Adrian Neville (R Evolution 2014); Bayley vs. Sasha Banks (Brooklyn 2015); Johnny Gargano vs. Tommaso Ciampa and Andrade "Cien" Almas vs. Gargano; Adam Cole vs. Gargano (XXV 2019).
- **Why it matters:** TakeOver made "developmental" a critical darling, launched the careers of a generation of stars, and repeatedly delivered match-of-the-year candidates on WrestleMania and SummerSlam weekends.

## AEW's "Big Four" (brief)
- **Slugs:** aew-double-or-nothing · aew-all-out · aew-revolution · aew-full-gear
- **Promotion:** AEW
- **First year:** All Elite Wrestling launched with **Double or Nothing** on May 25, 2019 (Las Vegas), followed that year by **All Out** (Chicago, Aug/Sep) and **Full Gear** (Nov); **Revolution** debuted in early 2020.
- **What they are:** AEW's four annual flagship PPVs — Double or Nothing (spring), All Out (late summer), Full Gear (fall) and Revolution (late winter/spring) — the pillars of its calendar.
- **Why they matter:** They established AEW as the most significant new national promotion in decades, reviving mainstream two-promotion competition. Revolution and Double or Nothing in particular are known for the "Iron Man"/blood-and-guts and Stadium Stampede spectacle matches that define AEW's identity.

# 2) CHAMPIONSHIP LINEAGES

Fifteen major titles across WWE/WWF, WCW, ECW, TNA and NXT.

---

## WWE Championship
- **Slug:** wwe-championship
- **Promotion:** WWF/WWE
- **What it is:** WWE's original and most historic world title, tracing to the WWWF in 1963.
- **Prestige / history:** Buddy Rogers is recognized as the inaugural champion (1963). Bruno Sammartino holds the record for total days as champion across two epic reigns; Bob Backlund had a near-six-year run. Hulk Hogan's 1984 win kicked off the national expansion. Renamed the "WWE Championship" in 2002 and briefly the "WWE World Heavyweight Championship" in the mid-2010s. Today it is the top prize on one brand alongside the World Heavyweight Championship.
- **Defining champions:** Bruno Sammartino, Bob Backlund, Hulk Hogan, "Stone Cold" Steve Austin, The Rock, Triple H, John Cena, CM Punk (434-day modern-era reign), Brock Lesnar.

## World Heavyweight Championship (WWE)
- **Slug:** world-heavyweight-championship
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **What it is:** A WWE world title that has existed in two distinct eras — the "big gold belt" (2002–2013) and a newly created lineage (2023–present).
- **Prestige / history:** The first version was introduced in 2002 (Triple H awarded it via Eric Bischoff) using the classic WCW/NWA "big gold belt," and was retired in December 2013 when Randy Orton unified it with the WWE Championship at TLC. Triple H revived a brand-new World Heavyweight Championship in 2023; Seth Rollins won a tournament to become its inaugural champion (crowned May 27, 2023), designed to keep a top title on Raw after Roman Reigns's unified reign kept the other belts off weekly TV.
- **Defining champions:** Triple H, Batista, Edge, Randy Orton, The Undertaker (2002–2013 era); Seth Rollins (inaugural 2023) leading the modern era.

## Universal / Undisputed WWE Championship
- **Slug:** universal-championship
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **What it is:** The title created for the Raw brand in 2016 that Roman Reigns unified into WWE's singular top prize.
- **Prestige / history:** Introduced in August 2016 with Finn Balor as inaugural champion (he vacated it the next night due to injury). Roman Reigns began a historic reign in August 2020 and, by defeating Brock Lesnar at WrestleMania 38 (2022), unified it with the WWE Championship as the "Undisputed WWE Universal Championship." Reigns held it for over 1,300 days until Cody Rhodes "finished the story" at WrestleMania 40 (2024); the belt was then renamed the **Undisputed WWE Championship**.
- **Defining champions:** Finn Balor (inaugural), Brock Lesnar, Roman Reigns (record-setting unified reign), Cody Rhodes.

## Intercontinental Championship
- **Slug:** intercontinental-championship
- **Promotion:** WWF/WWE
- **What it is:** WWE's premier secondary/"workhorse" title, established in 1979.
- **Prestige / history:** Pat Patterson is recognized as the inaugural champion. Long regarded as the belt for the best pure in-ring performers and a proving ground for future world champions. The Honky Tonk Man holds the longest single reign. Many all-time classics have been contested for it.
- **Defining champions:** Pat Patterson, Randy Savage, Ricky Steamboat (their WrestleMania III classic), The Honky Tonk Man, Bret Hart, Shawn Michaels, "Mr. Perfect" Curt Hennig, Chris Jericho.

## United States Championship
- **Slug:** united-states-championship
- **Promotion:** NWA / WCW / WWE
- **What it is:** A secondary singles title with deep NWA/Mid-Atlantic and WCW roots, brought into WWE in 2003.
- **Prestige / history:** Originated in Jim Crockett's Mid-Atlantic territory in 1975 (Harley Race is often cited as the first champion) and became WCW's top secondary belt. WWE acquired the lineage and reintroduced it in 2003. It has frequently been elevated by star champions — notably John Cena's "U.S. Open Challenge" run.
- **Defining champions:** Harley Race, Ric Flair, "Nature Boy" era stars, Lex Luger, Goldberg (WCW era); Eddie Guerrero, John Cena, Rey Mysterio (WWE era).

## WWE Tag Team Championship(s)
- **Slug:** wwe-tag-team-championship
- **Promotion:** WWF/WWE
- **What it is:** WWE's tag-team titles — historically the "World Tag Team Championship" (established 1971) and the "WWE Tag Team Championship" (created 2002), later unified.
- **Prestige / history:** The tag division has been a showcase for some of wrestling's greatest teams. The World and WWE tag titles co-existed across brands in the mid-2000s before unification; today Raw and SmackDown recognize tag championships used to anchor the division.
- **Defining champions:** The Road Warriors/Legion of Doom, Demolition, The Hart Foundation, The Dudley Boyz, Edge & Christian, The Hardy Boyz, The New Day (record-length reign), The Usos (record-length reign).

## Women's Championship (Raw / SmackDown / Divas)
- **Slug:** womens-championship
- **Promotion:** WWF/WWE
- **What it is:** WWE's top women's title(s), spanning the original Women's Championship, the Divas Championship, and today's Raw and SmackDown Women's Championships.
- **Prestige / history:** The original WWF Women's Championship dates to the 1950s and was dominated for decades by The Fabulous Moolah. The butterfly-emblem Divas Championship (2008–2016) marked the "Divas era." The 2016 "Women's Revolution" retired the Divas title and introduced the Raw and SmackDown Women's Championships, elevating women to main events, including the first women's WrestleMania main event (WrestleMania 35, 2019).
- **Defining champions:** The Fabulous Moolah, Trish Stratus, Lita (early eras); Charlotte Flair, Becky Lynch, Bayley and Sasha Banks / Mercedes Moné — the "Four Horsewomen" who drove the revolution.

## Money in the Bank (contract/briefcase)
- **Slug:** money-in-the-bank-briefcase
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **What it is:** Not a belt but a contract — a briefcase won in a ladder match guaranteeing a world-championship match at a time of the holder's choosing (roughly a one-year window).
- **Prestige / history:** Introduced at WrestleMania 21 (2005). Edge won the first and popularized the surprise "cash-in." The vast majority of cash-ins have succeeded, making the briefcase a roving championship threat; separate men's and women's contracts have been contested since 2017.
- **Defining champions/holders:** Edge (inaugural, first cash-in), CM Punk, Seth Rollins (WrestleMania 31 cash-in), Dolph Ziggler, Carmella (first women's winner), Sasha Banks / Mercedes Moné.

## WCW World Heavyweight Championship
- **Slug:** wcw-world-heavyweight-championship
- **Promotion:** WCW
- **What it is:** WCW's top world title, the centerpiece of the promotion during the Monday Night War.
- **Prestige / history:** Established as WCW's own world title in 1991 with Ric Flair recognized as the inaugural champion (evolving out of the NWA World lineage). It became the prize at the heart of the nWo era. WWE acquired it in 2001 and unified the lineage into its own titles. The "big gold belt" associated with it later became WWE's World Heavyweight Championship physical belt.
- **Defining champions:** Ric Flair, Sting, Hollywood Hulk Hogan, Goldberg (whose undefeated streak title win over Hogan drew a massive Nitro rating), Diamond Dallas Page, Booker T.

## WCW Cruiserweight Championship
- **Slug:** wcw-cruiserweight-championship
- **Promotion:** WCW
- **What it is:** A title for lighter-weight, high-flying wrestlers that defined WCW's most acclaimed in-ring division.
- **Prestige / history:** Introduced in 1996, it showcased a revolutionary blend of lucha libre, Japanese junior-heavyweight and high-flying styles that US audiences had rarely seen. The cruiserweight matches were frequently the best-worked bouts on WCW programming and a key reason critics rated the WCW product highly at its peak.
- **Defining champions:** Rey Mysterio Jr., Eddie Guerrero, Chris Jericho, Dean Malenko ("The Iceman"), Ultimo Dragon, Chris Benoit.

## ECW World Heavyweight Championship
- **Slug:** ecw-world-heavyweight-championship
- **Promotion:** ECW
- **What it is:** The top prize of Extreme Championship Wrestling.
- **Prestige / history:** Grew out of the Eastern/Extreme Championship Wrestling title lineage; the ECW World Championship identity crystallized in 1994 when Shane Douglas threw down the NWA World Title to declare himself ECW World Champion. It became the symbol of the hardcore movement and was carried by the cult promotion's biggest stars until ECW's 2001 closure; WWE later revived an ECW Championship for its 2006–2010 brand.
- **Defining champions:** Shane Douglas, The Sandman, Sabu, Taz, Mike Awesome, Rob Van Dam (RVD).

## TNA World Heavyweight Championship
- **Slug:** tna-world-heavyweight-championship
- **Promotion:** TNA
- **What it is:** TNA/Impact Wrestling's top world title.
- **Prestige / history:** In its early years TNA crowned champions under the NWA World Heavyweight Championship (AJ Styles was the first NWA-TNA World Champion in 2002). TNA established its **own** World Heavyweight Championship in 2007, with Kurt Angle recognized as the inaugural champion. The title anchored TNA's rise as the primary WWE alternative of the 2000s and remains the centerpiece through the promotion's post-2024 return to the "TNA" name.
- **Defining champions:** AJ Styles, Kurt Angle, Sting, Samoa Joe, Bobby Roode, Bully Ray.

## TNA X-Division Championship
- **Slug:** tna-x-division-championship
- **Promotion:** TNA
- **What it is:** TNA's signature high-flying, fast-paced division title, marketed with the slogan "it's not about weight limits, it's about no limits."
- **Prestige / history:** Established at TNA's 2002 launch, with AJ Styles as inaugural champion. The X-Division became TNA's defining innovation — spotlighting an athletic, innovative style (and multi-man Ultimate X matches) that distinguished the promotion and launched numerous international and independent stars.
- **Defining champions:** AJ Styles (inaugural and multiple reigns), Christopher Daniels, Samoa Joe, Jay Lethal, Austin Aries.

## NXT Championship
- **Slug:** nxt-championship
- **Promotion:** NXT (WWE)
- **What it is:** The top singles title of WWE's NXT brand.
- **Prestige / history:** Established in 2012, with Seth Rollins winning a tournament to become the inaugural champion. Under the TakeOver era it became one of the most prestigious titles in the company, held by wrestlers who went on to headline WrestleMania. It served as the crown jewel of the "black-and-gold" NXT that critics often rated above the main roster.
- **Defining champions:** Seth Rollins (inaugural), Finn Balor, Shinsuke Nakamura, Samoa Joe, Adam Cole, Tommaso Ciampa.

## NXT Women's Championship
- **Slug:** nxt-womens-championship
- **Promotion:** NXT (WWE)
- **What it is:** The top women's title of WWE's NXT brand and a driving force behind the Women's Revolution.
- **Prestige / history:** Established in 2013, with Paige winning a tournament to become inaugural champion. NXT became the proving ground where the "Four Horsewomen" and a generation of women's stars were developed, and its title matches (notably Bayley vs. Sasha Banks) are credited with changing how WWE presented women's wrestling.
- **Defining champions:** Paige (inaugural), Charlotte Flair, Bayley, Sasha Banks / Mercedes Moné, Asuka (record-length undefeated reign), Shayna Baszler.
