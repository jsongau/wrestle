# The Greatest & Most Significant Matches of the Modern Era (1996/97–Present)

A curated dataset of 30 landmark professional wrestling matches spanning WWF/WWE, WCW, ECW, TNA/Impact, ROH, and NXT. Star ratings reference Dave Meltzer / *Wrestling Observer Newsletter* (WON) and, where noted, the Cagematch community database (rating out of 10). Meltzer's ratings are cited as reported; he has revised several over the years, so where consensus differs it is flagged. Descriptions are original synthesis, not copied text.

**Legend**
- **Meltzer** = *Wrestling Observer* star rating (out of 5, historically; some post-2018 ratings exceed 5)
- **Cagematch** = community rating out of 10 (approximate where the live site was inaccessible)
- **MAT rating** = editorial call by this historian, out of 5, informed by critical consensus + historical weight

## 1. Bret Hart vs. "Stone Cold" Steve Austin
- **Slug:** bret-hart-vs-austin-wm13
- **Participants:** Bret "Hitman" Hart vs. "Stone Cold" Steve Austin
- **Event & Date:** WWF WrestleMania 13 — March 23, 1997 (Rosemont, IL)
- **Promotion:** WWF
- **Match type:** No-Disqualification Submission Match (special guest referee Ken Shamrock)
- **Result:** Bret Hart won (Austin passed out in the Sharpshooter; Shamrock stopped the match — Austin never submitted)
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~4.5★ (frequently cited among his WWF classics); Cagematch ~9.3–9.5
- **Why it matters:** The gold standard of the wrestling double-turn: Austin walked in the villain and left the biggest babyface in the industry, while Hart's cheap post-match assault flipped him heel. The image of a bloodied Austin roaring in the Sharpshooter rather than quitting became the visual thesis of the entire Attitude Era. It re-engineered both men's careers in a single bout and is routinely taught as the perfect example of psychology serving story.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock and WWE's YouTube (full match posted). Austin and Hart have both discussed it extensively in shoot interviews, including Chris Van Vliet sit-downs.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★★ — Peerless storytelling; arguably the most consequential single match of the modern era.

## 2. Shawn Michaels vs. The Undertaker (first-ever Hell in a Cell)
- **Slug:** hbk-vs-undertaker-badd-blood-1997
- **Participants:** Shawn Michaels vs. The Undertaker
- **Event & Date:** WWF In Your House 18: Badd Blood — October 5, 1997 (St. Louis, MO)
- **Promotion:** WWF
- **Match type:** Hell in a Cell (the first ever)
- **Result:** Shawn Michaels won (Kane made his debut, Tombstoned Undertaker, allowing HBK to steal the pin)
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~4.5–4.75★; Cagematch ~9.0
- **Why it matters:** The blueprint for the most iconic gimmick match WWE ever created, executed at a violence-and-drama level that later Cells rarely matched. HBK's bump off the cell and blade job, plus the fiery debut of Kane, gave the structure instant mythology. Everything from the cage's psychology to the "escape" spots originates here.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★½ — Genre-defining; the standard every Cell match is measured against.

## 3. Rey Mysterio Jr. vs. Eddie Guerrero (Title vs. Mask)
- **Slug:** rey-mysterio-vs-eddie-guerrero-halloween-havoc-1997
- **Participants:** Rey Mysterio Jr. vs. Eddie Guerrero
- **Event & Date:** WCW Halloween Havoc — October 26, 1997 (Las Vegas, NV)
- **Promotion:** WCW
- **Match type:** Title vs. Mask (WCW Cruiserweight Championship)
- **Result:** Rey Mysterio Jr. won the Cruiserweight Championship (kept his mask)
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~4.75★; Cagematch ~9.0
- **Why it matters:** Widely regarded as the greatest cruiserweight match in American wrestling history and the bout that proved US audiences would embrace lucha-style high flying. Rey's hurricanrana finish off Eddie's powerbomb attempt is one of the most replayed sequences of the 1990s. It legitimized WCW's cruiserweight division as must-see and made Rey a star.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE owns the WCW library; available on WWE Network/Peacock and posted in full on WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★★ — Flawless workrate showcase; the high-flying benchmark of the era.

## 4. The nWo Formation Angle (Hulk Hogan's heel turn)
- **Slug:** nwo-formation-bash-at-the-beach-1996
- **Participants:** The Outsiders (Scott Hall & Kevin Nash) & mystery partner vs. Sting, Lex Luger & Randy Savage
- **Event & Date:** WCW Bash at the Beach — July 7, 1996 (Daytona Beach, FL)
- **Promotion:** WCW
- **Match type:** Six-man tag (though the "third man" reveal is the point)
- **Result:** The Outsiders' side won after Hulk Hogan emerged, betrayed the WCW roster, and leg-dropped Savage — birthing the New World Order
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~2★ as a match (angle rated far higher for impact); not a workrate bout
- **Why it matters:** Included for historical weight, not in-ring quality — this is arguably the single most influential angle of the Monday Night War. Hogan turning heel after 15 years as America's hero, littering the ring with trash, launched the nWo and sent WCW to 83 straight weeks of ratings dominance over Raw. It rewrote the economics and creative direction of the entire industry. (Note: technically 1996, but the defining "modern era" storyline the era is built on.)
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock (WCW library); the promo segment is heavily featured in WWE documentaries.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★½ (for significance) — As a match, forgettable; as a moment, era-defining.

## 5. Sting vs. Hollywood Hulk Hogan
- **Slug:** sting-vs-hogan-starrcade-1997
- **Participants:** Sting vs. Hollywood Hulk Hogan
- **Event & Date:** WCW Starrcade — December 28, 1997 (Washington, D.C.)
- **Promotion:** WCW
- **Match type:** Singles — WCW World Heavyweight Championship
- **Result:** Sting won the WCW World Championship (after a controversial referee count and a Bret Hart-ordered restart)
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~2–2.5★ (celebrated build, botched execution)
- **Why it matters:** The 18-month payoff to WCW's best-ever slow-burn story — the silent, rafter-dwelling "Crow" Sting hunting the nWo — is the most anticipated match in company history. The finish was infamously bungled (a fast count that wasn't clearly fast, Nick Patrick, Bret Hart's involvement), a booking failure that many historians mark as the beginning of WCW's decline. It endures as the ultimate lesson in how a perfect build can be undone by a muddled ending.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock (WCW library).
- **MAT rating:** ★★★½ — Historic and emotionally huge, dragged down by its self-inflicted finish.

## 6. Goldberg vs. Hollywood Hulk Hogan
- **Slug:** goldberg-vs-hogan-nitro-1998
- **Participants:** Bill Goldberg vs. Hollywood Hulk Hogan
- **Event & Date:** WCW Monday Nitro — July 6, 1998 (Georgia Dome, Atlanta, GA)
- **Promotion:** WCW
- **Match type:** Singles — WCW World Heavyweight Championship
- **Result:** Goldberg won the WCW World Championship (Spear + Jackhammer), continuing his undefeated streak
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~3★
- **Why it matters:** The apex of the Goldberg phenomenon — a genuinely great sports-entertainment moment where an undefeated monster dethroned Hogan in front of 40,000+ fans. Famously given away for free on Nitro rather than a pay-per-view, it drew a massive number and is the textbook example of "hot-shotting" a marquee match for a short-term rating at the cost of long-term money. It cemented Goldberg's streak as one of the era's biggest attractions.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock (WCW library), WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★ — Not a mat classic, but one of the most electric moments WCW ever produced.

## 7. Terry Funk vs. Sabu (Barbed Wire)
- **Slug:** terry-funk-vs-sabu-born-to-be-wired-1997
- **Participants:** Terry Funk vs. Sabu
- **Event & Date:** ECW Born to Be Wired — August 9, 1997 (Hammonton, NJ)
- **Promotion:** ECW
- **Match type:** Barbed Wire ropes match — ECW World Heavyweight Championship
- **Result:** Terry Funk won the ECW World Heavyweight Championship
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~3.75–4★ (as a spectacle/hardcore piece)
- **Why it matters:** The defining image of ECW's ultraviolent identity — barbed wire replacing the ropes, wrestlers hopelessly tangled, Sabu tearing open his bicep and taping it shut mid-match to continue. A 53-year-old Funk winning the title in this environment showcased the courage and lunacy that made ECW a cult phenomenon. It is the promotion's most-cited hardcore classic.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE owns the ECW library; available on WWE Network/Peacock.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★ — Not a technical bout, but the purest distillation of what ECW was.

## 8. TLC II (Edge & Christian vs. Hardy Boyz vs. Dudley Boyz)
- **Slug:** tlc-2-wrestlemania-x-seven-2001
- **Participants:** Edge & Christian vs. The Hardy Boyz vs. The Dudley Boyz (with Rhyno, Spike Dudley, Lita)
- **Event & Date:** WWF WrestleMania X-Seven — April 1, 2001 (Houston, TX)
- **Promotion:** WWF
- **Match type:** Tables, Ladders & Chairs — WWF Tag Team Championship
- **Result:** Edge & Christian won the WWF Tag Team Championship
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~4.25★; Cagematch ~8.7
- **Why it matters:** The crown jewel of the ladder-match arms race between the three teams that redefined WWF tag wrestling. Iconic spots — Edge's spear on Jeff Hardy in mid-air, Jeff's dangling Swanton, the run-ins and human carnage — turned car-crash choreography into art. It set the template for the entire TLC lineage and the modern multi-man ladder match.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube (full match).
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★½ — The definitive spot-fest; six careers put on the line for one unforgettable sprint.

## 9. The Rock vs. "Stone Cold" Steve Austin
- **Slug:** rock-vs-austin-wm-x-seven-2001
- **Participants:** The Rock vs. "Stone Cold" Steve Austin
- **Event & Date:** WWF WrestleMania X-Seven — April 1, 2001 (Houston, TX)
- **Promotion:** WWF
- **Match type:** No-Disqualification — WWF Championship
- **Result:** Steve Austin won the WWF Championship (aligning with Vince McMahon in a shocking heel turn)
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer 4.5★; Cagematch ~8.8
- **Why it matters:** The main event of what many call the greatest WrestleMania ever, pitting the two biggest stars of the Attitude Era at the absolute peak of the boom. The stunning heel turn — Austin shaking hands with his archenemy McMahon and battering Rock with a chair — is one of the most debated creative decisions in company history. It closed the Attitude Era's golden age on a shocking, blood-soaked note.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★½ — Two icons at their zenith; the turn is divisive but the drama is immense.

## 10. Kurt Angle vs. Chris Benoit
- **Slug:** angle-vs-benoit-royal-rumble-2003
- **Participants:** Kurt Angle vs. Chris Benoit
- **Event & Date:** WWE Royal Rumble — January 19, 2003 (Boston, MA)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Singles — WWE Championship
- **Result:** Kurt Angle retained the WWE Championship
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer 4.75★; Cagematch ~9.0
- **Why it matters:** A pure wrestling clinic between two of the greatest technicians of their generation — a chain of counters, suplexes, and submission reversals that Angle himself has called the best match he ever had. It gave the smaller, submission-based Benoit a marquee showcase and demonstrated that main-event WWE could still be built on mat mastery in the sports-entertainment era. Widely held up as one of the finest technical matches in WWE history.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★★ — As pure a wrestling match as WWE has produced this century.

## 11. Brock Lesnar vs. Kurt Angle
- **Slug:** lesnar-vs-angle-wm19-2003
- **Participants:** Brock Lesnar vs. Kurt Angle
- **Event & Date:** WWE WrestleMania XIX — March 30, 2003 (Seattle, WA)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Singles — WWE Championship
- **Result:** Brock Lesnar won the WWE Championship
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~4.25★; Cagematch ~8.5
- **Why it matters:** A superb amateur-based main event between two former NCAA/Olympic-caliber wrestlers, remembered as much for its quality as for its finish: Lesnar nearly broke his neck landing a botched shooting star press, then gutted through to win. It capped Lesnar's meteoric first-year rise and showcased Angle wrestling despite a badly injured neck. The near-tragedy adds a legendary footnote to an already excellent bout.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★ — Elite athleticism and a moment of genuine peril seared into fans' memory.

## 12. Chris Benoit vs. Triple H vs. Shawn Michaels
- **Slug:** benoit-vs-triple-h-vs-hbk-wm20-2004
- **Participants:** Chris Benoit vs. Triple H vs. Shawn Michaels
- **Event & Date:** WWE WrestleMania XX — March 14, 2004 (Madison Square Garden, New York, NY)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Triple Threat — World Heavyweight Championship
- **Result:** Chris Benoit won the World Heavyweight Championship (submitting Triple H with the Crippler Crossface)
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer 4.75★; Cagematch ~9.0
- **Why it matters:** The underdog-makes-good story of the era: a career mid-card technician finally reaching the mountaintop in the main event of WrestleMania at MSG. The emotional coda — Benoit celebrating with best friend Eddie Guerrero, both new world champions — is one of the most beloved images in WWE history (though its legacy is now complicated by later events). The match itself is a masterclass in three-way pacing and false finishes.
- **Video:** Available but suppressed — WWE has largely removed Benoit's content from official platforms; the match is rarely streamed on Network/Peacock and not promoted, though it circulates elsewhere.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★★ — In-ring, an all-time WrestleMania main event; its availability and legacy are uniquely complicated.

## 13. Shawn Michaels vs. Kurt Angle
- **Slug:** hbk-vs-angle-wm21-2005
- **Participants:** Shawn Michaels vs. Kurt Angle
- **Event & Date:** WWE WrestleMania 21 — April 3, 2005 (Los Angeles, CA)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Singles (dream match)
- **Result:** Kurt Angle won (Michaels submitted to the ankle lock)
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer 4.75★; Cagematch ~9.0
- **Why it matters:** A long-awaited "dream match" between the best pure wrestler (Angle) and the best all-around performer (Michaels) of the era, and it delivered a near-flawless blend of drama and technique. HBK collapsing into the ankle lock after a valiant struggle is one of his finest selling performances. It spawned a celebrated rivalry (rematched at Vengeance 2005) and stands as a WrestleMania in-ring high point.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★★ — Two masters, zero wasted motion.

## 14. The Undertaker vs. Kurt Angle
- **Slug:** undertaker-vs-angle-no-way-out-2006
- **Participants:** The Undertaker vs. Kurt Angle
- **Event & Date:** WWE No Way Out — February 19, 2006 (Baltimore, MD)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Singles — World Heavyweight Championship
- **Result:** Kurt Angle retained the World Heavyweight Championship
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer 4.75★; Cagematch ~8.8

The counter-heavy struggle between Angle's technical assault and Undertaker's size and resilience produced a tense near-fall symphony. ## 15. The Undertaker vs. Shawn Michaels
- **Slug:** undertaker-vs-hbk-wm25-2009
- **Participants:** The Undertaker vs. Shawn Michaels
- **Event & Date:** WWE WrestleMania XXV — April 5, 2009 (Houston, TX)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Singles — The Streak on the line (Undertaker 16–0)
- **Result:** The Undertaker won (extending the WrestleMania Streak to 17–0)
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer 4.75★; Cagematch ~9.6
- **Why it matters:** Regularly voted the greatest WrestleMania match ever and a strong contender for greatest WWE match of all time. Two 40-something legends built a near-perfect drama of near-falls, capped by Undertaker's terrifying botched-then-recovered dive over the ropes. Meltzer withheld the fifth star (he later explained the dive scare cost it), but critical and fan consensus treats it as a five-star classic.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★★ — The high-water mark for WrestleMania in-ring storytelling.

## 16. The Undertaker vs. Shawn Michaels (Streak vs. Career)
- **Slug:** undertaker-vs-hbk-wm26-2010
- **Participants:** The Undertaker vs. Shawn Michaels
- **Event & Date:** WWE WrestleMania XXVI — March 28, 2010 (Glendale, AZ)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** No Disqualification — Streak vs. Career
- **Result:** The Undertaker won (18–0); Shawn Michaels was forced to retire
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer 4.5★; Cagematch ~9.4
- **Why it matters:** The rare sequel that justified itself, adding the stakes of HBK's career to the Streak's mystique. Michaels' defiant slit-throat taunt and Undertaker's reluctant, almost mournful Tombstone gave the finish real gravity, sending one of the greatest ever into retirement on the biggest stage. It's the emotional bookend to the WM25 masterpiece.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★½ — A worthy, emotionally devastating farewell to HBK.

## 17. CM Punk vs. John Cena
- **Slug:** cm-punk-vs-cena-mitb-2011
- **Participants:** CM Punk vs. John Cena
- **Event & Date:** WWE Money in the Bank — July 17, 2011 (Allstate Arena, Chicago, IL)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Singles — WWE Championship (Punk's contract expiring; hometown stakes)
- **Result:** CM Punk won the WWE Championship (and left the company with the title)
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer 4.75★ (widely listed as a five-star classic; some later tallies say 5★); Cagematch ~9.4
- **Why it matters:** The payoff to the "pipebomb" summer that reinvigorated WWE — a rabid Chicago crowd behind hometown antihero Punk against the company standard-bearer Cena, with the belt (and Punk's future) genuinely uncertain. The white-hot atmosphere and pitch-perfect near-falls made it feel like the most important match in years. It's the defining match of Punk's WWE run and one of the most beloved of the 2010s.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube. Punk has revisited the night in numerous interviews (including Chris Van Vliet).
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★★ — The most emotionally charged WWE match of its decade.

## 18. The Undertaker vs. Triple H (End of an Era)
- **Slug:** undertaker-vs-triple-h-wm28-2012
- **Participants:** The Undertaker vs. Triple H (special guest referee Shawn Michaels)
- **Event & Date:** WWE WrestleMania XXVIII — April 1, 2012 (Miami, FL)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Hell in a Cell — The Streak on the line
- **Result:** The Undertaker won (20–0)
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer 4.75★; Cagematch ~9.2
- **Why it matters:** The final chapter of the Undertaker/HBK/Triple H trilogy, staged inside the Cell with Michaels as conflicted referee — a three-way character drama disguised as a brawl. The theme of aging warriors giving everything, and the three men embracing on the ramp afterward, framed it as a literal "end of an era." A masterful example of storytelling elevating a physically limited (by then) Undertaker.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★½ — Emotional, brutal, and beautifully choreographed drama.

## 19. Daniel Bryan vs. John Cena
- **Slug:** bryan-vs-cena-summerslam-2013
- **Participants:** Daniel Bryan vs. John Cena
- **Event & Date:** WWE SummerSlam — August 18, 2013 (Los Angeles, CA)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Singles — WWE Championship (special guest referee Triple H)
- **Result:** Daniel Bryan won the WWE Championship cleanly — then Randy Orton cashed in Money in the Bank (after Triple H turned and Pedigreed Bryan) to win the title
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer 4.5★; Cagematch ~8.8
- **Why it matters:** Cena, often accused of coasting, delivered one of his career-best performances to make underdog Bryan look like a world-beater. Bryan's clean win, immediately snatched away by the Orton cash-in and the birth of "The Authority," launched the year-long "Yes Movement" that culminated at WrestleMania 30. It's the ignition point of one of WWE's great modern storylines.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★½ — A star-making underdog performance and a franchise-launching finish.

## 20. Daniel Bryan's WrestleMania XXX Night (the "Yes Movement" payoff)
- **Slug:** daniel-bryan-wm30-2014
- **Participants:** (Opener) Daniel Bryan vs. Triple H; (Main event) Daniel Bryan vs. Batista vs. Randy Orton
- **Event & Date:** WWE WrestleMania XXX — April 6, 2014 (New Orleans, LA)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Singles (opener) + Triple Threat for the WWE World Heavyweight Championship (main event)
- **Result:** Bryan beat Triple H to earn his main-event spot, then won the WWE World Heavyweight Championship in the main event (submitting Batista with the Yes Lock)
- **Critical rating:** Opener (vs. HHH) Meltzer ~4.5★; main event Meltzer ~4★; Cagematch (opener) ~8.9
- **Why it matters:** The complete payoff to the grassroots "Yes Movement" — the anti-corporate, undersized fan favorite the machine tried to hold down finally conquering it twice in one night, first the boss (Triple H) then the two hand-picked stars. The image of a beaten-and-taped Bryan standing tall amid a sea of "Yes!" chants is one of the era's defining feel-good moments. Rarely has fan will so visibly overridden a company's plans.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube. Bryan has discussed the run in numerous interviews, including with Chris Van Vliet.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★½ — The opener is a mat classic; the night as a whole is one of WWE's great emotional triumphs.

## 21. Seth Rollins' Money in the Bank Cash-In
- **Slug:** rollins-cash-in-wm31-2015
- **Participants:** Brock Lesnar vs. Roman Reigns → turned into a Triple Threat by Seth Rollins
- **Event & Date:** WWE WrestleMania 31 — March 29, 2015 (Santa Clara, CA)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Singles for the WWE World Heavyweight Championship, converted mid-match into a Triple Threat via MITB cash-in
- **Result:** Seth Rollins won the WWE World Heavyweight Championship (pinning Reigns) — the first Money in the Bank cash-in during a WrestleMania main event
- **Critical rating:** Lesnar/Reigns portion Meltzer ~4.25★; the cash-in moment rated far higher for impact; Cagematch ~8.7
- **Why it matters:** A genuinely shocking swerve that rescued a main event the crowd had soured on (fans rejected Reigns; loved a rampaging Lesnar). Rollins sprinting out to cash in and steal the title turned a divisive show into a triumphant one and was instantly dubbed "the Heist of the Century." It remains the gold standard for the Money in the Bank concept's dramatic potential.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★ — The match plus the swerve equals one of WrestleMania's most memorable endings.

## 22. Ronda Rousey vs. Charlotte Flair vs. Becky Lynch

"The Man" Becky Lynch, riding one of the hottest fan-driven pushes in years, capped her ascent against the crossover star (Rousey) and the company's chosen ace (Charlotte). The controversial pinfall finish couldn't diminish the historic significance of women closing the biggest show of the year.

## 23. AJ Styles vs. Christopher Daniels vs. Samoa Joe
- **Slug:** styles-vs-daniels-vs-joe-unbreakable-2005
- **Participants:** AJ Styles vs. Christopher Daniels vs. Samoa Joe
- **Event & Date:** TNA Unbreakable — September 11, 2005 (Orlando, FL)
- **Promotion:** TNA (Total Nonstop Action)
- **Match type:** Triple Threat — TNA X-Division Championship
- **Result:** AJ Styles won the X-Division Championship
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer 5★ (TNA's first five-star match); Cagematch ~9.2

It's frequently named the greatest match in TNA history and a landmark for American independent-style wrestling reaching a national stage. It made stars of all three men.

## 24. Samoa Joe vs. CM Punk (Joe vs. Punk II)
- **Slug:** samoa-joe-vs-cm-punk-roh-2004
- **Participants:** Samoa Joe vs. CM Punk
- **Event & Date:** ROH World Title Classic / "Joe vs. Punk II" — October 16, 2004 (Chicago Ridge, IL)
- **Promotion:** Ring of Honor (ROH)
- **Match type:** Singles — ROH World Championship, 60-minute time limit
- **Result:** Time-limit draw (Joe retained the ROH World Championship)
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer 5★ (ROH's first five-star match); Cagematch ~9.3
- **Why it matters:** The match that legitimized the American independent scene as a home for world-class wrestling, and the second (and best) chapter of the Joe/Punk trilogy that defined early ROH. A full hour of escalating drama that never sagged, it made both men bona fide stars and is a foundational text for the indie boom that would later feed WWE, NXT, and AEW. Meltzer awarding it five stars validated the entire indie movement.
- **Video:** Yes — the ROH library is now owned by AEW/Tony Khan (HonorClub); the match circulates widely online. Both men have reflected on the trilogy in interviews.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★★ — The Rosetta Stone of the modern American independent scene.

## 25. Sami Zayn vs. Adrian Neville
- **Slug:** sami-zayn-vs-neville-takeover-revolution-2014
- **Participants:** Sami Zayn vs. Adrian Neville
- **Event & Date:** NXT TakeOver: R Evolution — December 11, 2014 (Winter Park, FL)
- **Promotion:** WWE NXT
- **Match type:** Singles — NXT Championship
