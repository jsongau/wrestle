## 2. Partner-by-Partner Breakdown

### Brian Pillman — *The Hollywood Blonds* (1992–1993)
- **Titles won together:** WCW World Tag Team Championship (1993).
- **Notable matches:** Title win over Ricky Steamboat & Shane Douglas (March 1993); retaining vs. "Dos Hombres" at Slamboree 1993; feud with Ric Flair & Arn Anderson (the mocking "A Flair for the Old" talk-show parody segments).
- **Dynamic:** A genuine, cohesive best-friends heel team — cocky, brash, and one of the best-worked tandems in early-'90s WCW. Real chemistry, not a forced pairing.

### Shawn Michaels (Spring 1997)
- **Titles won together:** WWF World Tag Team Championship (May 1997).
- **Notable matches:** The May 26, 1997 *Raw* title win over Owen Hart & British Bulldog (frequently cited as an all-time great TV tag match).
- **Dynamic:** Two alpha egos and locker-room rivals reluctantly aligned against the Hart Foundation — an uneasy, tension-filled alliance rather than a true team.

### Dude Love / Mick Foley (July–September 1997)
- **Titles won together:** WWF World Tag Team Championship (July 1997).
- **Notable matches:** The July 14, 1997 *Raw* vacant-title win, built around Foley's surprise reveal as Austin's mystery partner.
- **Dynamic:** An odd-couple pairing — Foley's goofy, tie-dyed "Dude Love" gimmick bolted onto the no-nonsense Austin. Comedy-tinged but effective.

### The Undertaker (Mid-1998)
- **Titles won together:** WWF World Tag Team Championship (July–August 1998).
- **Notable matches:** Fully Loaded 1998 title win over Kane & Mankind; the Aug 10, 1998 *Raw* rematch loss.
- **Dynamic:** Enemies-forced-to-team — Austin and Undertaker were simultaneously feuding over the WWF Championship (amid Vince McMahon's machinations), so the partnership was a temporary, distrustful alliance.

### Triple H — *The Two-Man Power Trip* (Spring 2001)
- **Titles won together:** WWF World Tag Team Championship (April–May 2001).
- **Notable matches:** Backlash 2001 win over The Undertaker & Kane; the May 21, 2001 *Raw* loss to Benoit & Jericho (Triple H's quad tear).
- **Dynamic:** A dominant super-heel alliance of two top main-eventers (post-WrestleMania X-Seven heel Austin + Triple H), backed by McMahon — briefly the most powerful act in the company, holding singles and tag gold at once.

## 4. What His Tag Work Shows

Austin's tag résumé is a study in **versatility and range as a partner**. He built a genuine, chemistry-rich team early with Brian Pillman (the Hollywood Blonds), then in the WWF repeatedly won gold with **rivals and mismatched partners** rather than natural allies: an uneasy Shawn Michaels, the comedic Dude Love, his on-and-off nemesis The Undertaker, and finally a dominant heel super-team with Triple H. That he could carry a title reign whether cast as a cohesive teammate, a reluctant enemy-turned-partner, or one half of a monster heel duo speaks to his ring generalship and adaptability. Notably, **most of his tag titles came with men he was feuding with or oddly paired against** — the mark of a performer whose in-ring presence made almost any pairing credible.

## Verification Notes / Flags
- **WCW win date:** March 2, 1993 (taping) per F4Wonline; aired March 27, 1993. Same title change, dated differently by source.
- **Austin & Michaels reign end:** Titles were **vacated (not lost in the ring)** due to Michaels' injury in July 1997; the exact injury description varies (knee/back) between sources. There was **no separate Owen/Bulldog reign** in between — Owen & Bulldog were simply the opponents in the subsequent vacant-title match.
- **WWF tag reign count:** 4, consistent across all consulted sources.
- All other dates, opponents, and results above were consistent across at least two independent sources (WWE.com corroborates the May 26, 1997 and Fully Loaded 1998 matches directly).
