## 1. Pre-"Stone Cold" Ring Names / Personas

### 1a. "Stunning" Steve Austin (WCW, 1991–1995)

- **Promotion:** World Championship Wrestling (WCW)
- **Years:** May 1991 – September 15, 1995
- **Look / Gimmick:** Long bleached-blond hair, tanned, arrogant "pretty boy" heel and crisp technical wrestler. Accompanied early on by valet Vivacious Veronica, then by manager/valet Lady Blossom. Used the Stun Gun as a signature finisher.
- **Titles:**
  - WCW World Television Championship — 2 reigns (first won June 3, 1991, defeating Bobby Eaton)
  - WCW United States Heavyweight Championship — 2 reigns (won at Starrcade, December 1993)
  - WCW World Tag Team Championship — with Brian Pillman as the **Hollywood Blonds** (won March 27, 1993)
  - NWA World Tag Team Championship — shared with Pillman during the unified reign
- **Hollywood Blonds:** Cocky blond tag team formed September 1992 with Brian Pillman; held the unified NWA/WCW World Tag Team titles for roughly five months before injuries and creative decisions split the team in late 1993.
- **Notable matches / feuds:** Extended feud with Ricky "The Dragon" Steamboat over the US Championship (lost the belt to Steamboat at Clash of the Champions XX, September 1992); rivalry with Dustin Rhodes; Hollywood Blonds vs. Steamboat & Shane Douglas and vs. Ricky Steamboat & the Horsemen.
- **Firing / connection to Stone Cold:** Eric Bischoff fired Austin on September 15, 1995 (delivered via FedEx while Austin was rehabbing a torn triceps), deeming him unmarketable and "not a draw." That firing pushed Austin to ECW and then WWF, where he reinvented himself. The chip-on-the-shoulder resentment from being fired directly fueled the anti-authority Stone Cold character.

### 1b. The Ringmaster (WWF, 1995–1996)

- **Promotion:** World Wrestling Federation (WWF)
- **Years:** Debuted in WWF December 18, 1995 (first aired January 8, 1996); dropped the gimmick by early 1996.
- **Look / Gimmick:** Austin's first WWF character — a straightforward, technically-gifted heel repackaged as "The Ringmaster," managed by "Million Dollar Man" Ted DiBiase. A bland, manager-driven gimmick Austin openly disliked.
- **Titles:** Million Dollar Championship (awarded/managed by DiBiase).
- **Notable matches / feuds:** DiBiase mouthpiece act; early WWF programming against undercard talent.
- **Connection to / transition into Stone Cold:** Austin quickly rejected the Ringmaster concept. He shaved his head (partly inspired by Bruce Willis in *Pulp Fiction*), grew a goatee, and adopted a new name — the "Stone Cold" moniker suggested when his then-wife Jeanie told him to drink his tea before it got "stone cold." The character crystallized at **King of the Ring 1996** (June 23, 1996), where Austin won the tournament by defeating Jake "The Snake" Roberts and, mocking Roberts' born-again Christian gimmick, cut the legendary promo: **"Austin 3:16 says I just whipped your ass!"** — closing with "and that's the bottom line, 'cause Stone Cold said so." "Austin 3:16" became one of wrestling's most iconic catchphrases and best-selling merchandise lines, launching the Stone Cold era.

### 1c. ECW Stint — "Superstar" / "Superstar" Steve Austin (ECW, 1995–1996)

- **Promotion:** Extreme Championship Wrestling (ECW)
- **Years:** September 1995 – early 1996 (debuted at Gangsta's Paradise, September 18, 1995)
- **Look / Gimmick:** Wrestled largely under his own name, "Superstar" Steve Austin — a promo-driven run more than an in-ring one, as he was still recovering from his triceps injury. Paul Heyman paid him roughly $500/night to cut razor-sharp interviews.
- **Connection to Stone Cold:** This is where Austin found his voice. Heyman gave him the mic freedom to vent real bitterness — most famously mocking WCW and Eric Bischoff in his "Monday Nyquil" (parody of Monday Nitro) and "Superstar" promos. Those unfiltered, resentment-fueled interviews are widely credited as the proving ground for the attitude and cadence that became Stone Cold.

## 2. The "Stone Cold" Persona & the Attitude Era

- **Character:** A beer-swilling, foul-mouthed, anti-authority antihero — the ultimate blue-collar rebel who flipped off his boss, drank Steveweisers in the ring, and lived by "Don't Trust Anybody." His finisher was the Stone Cold Stunner.
- **The McMahon feud:** Austin's war with WWF chairman Mr. McMahon (Vince McMahon) — the everyman employee vs. the tyrant boss — became the defining storyline of the era and a template for pro-wrestling booking.
- **"Austin 3:16":** Coined at King of the Ring 1996; became the signature catchphrase and best-selling merchandise of the boom.
- **Peak / significance:** Widely regarded as the most popular star of WWF's late-1990s Attitude Era and a key driver of WWF's victory in the Monday Night War. Accomplishments include 6× WWF Champion, 2× Intercontinental Champion, 4× Tag Team Champion, and 3× Royal Rumble winner (1997, 1998, 2001), plus King of the Ring 1996.
- **Neck injury:** At SummerSlam 1997, Owen Hart botched a sit-out (tombstone-style) piledriver, dropping Austin on his head — causing a bruised spinal cord and temporary paralysis. The lingering damage shortened his career.
- **Retirement:** Retired from full-time in-ring competition after WrestleMania XIX (March 30, 2003), losing to The Rock, due to accumulated neck and knee injuries. Inducted into the WWE Hall of Fame in 2009.
- **2022 return:** Came out of a 19-year in-ring retirement for a "no-holds-barred" match against Kevin Owens at **WrestleMania 38 (Night 1, April 2, 2022)** in his home state of Texas, which Austin won — followed by a Stunner-fest to close the night.
