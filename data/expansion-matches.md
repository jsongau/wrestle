# The Greatest & Most Significant Matches — Expansion Set (1997–Present)

A second curated dataset of 35 landmark professional wrestling matches, designed to sit alongside the original 30 in `matches.md` **without repeating any of them**. Coverage deliberately widens the net across WWF/WWE, WCW, ECW, TNA/Impact, ROH, NJPW, WWE NXT, and modern WWE and AEW — including a heavy dose of 2021–2026 classics.

As in the companion file, star ratings reference Dave Meltzer / *Wrestling Observer Newsletter* (WON) and, where noted, the Cagematch community database (rating out of 10). Meltzer famously "broke the scale" in 2017, so several NJPW/AEW entries carry ratings above the old 5★ ceiling; those are reported as he issued them. All descriptions are original synthesis, not copied text.

**Legend**
- **Meltzer** = *Wrestling Observer* star rating (historically out of 5; post-2017 ratings can exceed 5)
- **Cagematch** = community rating out of 10 (approximate where the live site was inaccessible)
- **MAT rating** = editorial call by this historian, out of 5, informed by critical consensus + historical weight

## 31. The Rock vs. "Stone Cold" Steve Austin (WrestleMania XIX)
- **Slug:** rock-vs-austin-wm19-2003
- **Participants:** The Rock vs. "Stone Cold" Steve Austin
- **Event & Date:** WWE WrestleMania XIX — March 30, 2003 (Seattle, WA)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Singles (the rubber match of their WrestleMania trilogy)
- **Result:** The Rock won (his first pinfall over Austin at WrestleMania, after three Rock Bottoms)
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~4.5★; Cagematch ~8.6
- **Why it matters:** The emotional close of the greatest one-on-one WrestleMania rivalry, and — though nobody publicly knew it at the time — Austin's retirement match. Rock, now a Hollywood-bound heel, finally got his win, then showed rare sincerity by acknowledging Austin's greatness. Knowing Austin's neck ended his career here recasts the bout as a quiet farewell to the Attitude Era's defining star.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★½ — Two icons closing the book with craft and unexpected poignancy.

## 32. Money in the Bank Ladder Match (the original)
- **Slug:** money-in-the-bank-ladder-wm21-2005
- **Participants:** Edge vs. Chris Benoit vs. Chris Jericho vs. Christian vs. Kane vs. Shelton Benjamin
- **Event & Date:** WWE WrestleMania 21 — April 3, 2005 (Los Angeles, CA)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Six-man Ladder Match (inaugural Money in the Bank; winner earns a guaranteed world title shot)
- **Result:** Edge won the first Money in the Bank briefcase
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~4.5★; Cagematch ~8.5
- **Why it matters:** The birth of a concept that reshaped modern WWE booking — a briefcase guaranteeing a title shot any time, any place, which turned the cash-in into one of the company's most reliable dramatic devices (see Rollins at WM31 in the companion file). Shelton Benjamin's run-up-the-ladder clothesline became an instant highlight, and Edge's win launched him toward main-event stardom. It spawned an annual match and, eventually, its own pay-per-view.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★½ — A concept-defining spotfest that changed how WWE tells title stories.

## 33. Eddie Guerrero vs. Brock Lesnar
- **Slug:** eddie-guerrero-vs-lesnar-no-way-out-2004
- **Participants:** Eddie Guerrero vs. Brock Lesnar
- **Event & Date:** WWE No Way Out — February 15, 2004 (Daly City / San Francisco, CA)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Singles — WWE Championship
- **Result:** Eddie Guerrero won the WWE Championship (his first and only world title, with help from a chair and Goldberg's distraction)
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~4.5★; Cagematch ~8.4
- **Why it matters:** The coronation of one of the most beloved performers of the era — a recovering-addict everyman finally reaching the mountaintop against a near-unbeatable monster. Eddie's tearful celebration is one of WWE's most cherished feel-good moments, made unbearably poignant by his death 21 months later. The match itself is a superb David-vs-Goliath built on Eddie's cunning.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★½ — Great in isolation; historically and emotionally immense.

## 34. Edge vs. John Cena (TLC)
- **Slug:** edge-vs-cena-tlc-unforgiven-2006
- **Participants:** Edge vs. John Cena
- **Event & Date:** WWE Unforgiven — September 17, 2006 (Toronto, ON, Canada)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Tables, Ladders & Chairs — WWE Championship
- **Result:** Edge won the WWE Championship (in his Toronto hometown)
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~4.25★; Cagematch ~8.3
- **Why it matters:** The peak of the Cena/Edge rivalry that defined WWE's mid-2000s main-event scene, staged in a rabid pro-Edge Canadian building. Cena's spear-through-two-tables off the ladder and Edge's title-winning tumble made it a car-crash classic with real heat. It cemented Edge as the "Rated-R Superstar" top heel and gave Cena one of his most acclaimed early main events.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★½ — Blistering hometown drama and one of Cena's best pre-2010 outings.

## 35. John Cena vs. Umaga (Last Man Standing)
- **Slug:** cena-vs-umaga-last-man-standing-royal-rumble-2007
- **Participants:** John Cena vs. Umaga (with Armando Estrada)
- **Event & Date:** WWE Royal Rumble — January 28, 2007 (San Antonio, TX)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Last Man Standing — WWE Championship
- **Result:** John Cena retained the WWE Championship (choking out Umaga with the ring ropes so he couldn't answer the ten-count)
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~4.5★; Cagematch ~8.6
- **Why it matters:** Widely cited as one of Cena's finest matches and the definitive proof that he could carry a brutal, blood-soaked slugfest against a monster. The finish — Cena wrapping the top rope around Umaga's throat to end it — was an unusually vicious, resourceful babyface win. It rehabilitated Umaga as a credible force and silenced a chunk of the "Cena can't work" criticism.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★½ — A savage, smartly-worked epic; a top-tier Cena performance.

## 37. John Cena vs. Shawn Michaels (Raw, London)
- **Slug:** cena-vs-hbk-raw-london-2007
- **Participants:** John Cena vs. Shawn Michaels
- **Event & Date:** WWE Monday Night Raw — April 23, 2007 (London, England)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Singles — WWE Championship (WrestleMania 23 rematch)
- **Result:** John Cena retained the WWE Championship (submitting HBK to the STFU after ~55 minutes)

## 40. The 2001 Royal Rumble Match

## 41. AJ Styles vs. John Cena
- **Slug:** aj-styles-vs-cena-summerslam-2016

- **Event & Date:** WWE SummerSlam — August 21, 2016 (Brooklyn, NY)

- **Match type:** Singles (dream match)
- **Result:** AJ Styles won (clean, pinning Cena after a Styles Clash and a low blow into a Styles Clash)

- **MAT rating:** ★★★★½ — A pitch-perfect dream match with a franchise-defining result.

## 44. Cody Rhodes vs. Seth Rollins (Hell in a Cell)
- **Slug:** cody-rhodes-vs-seth-rollins-hell-in-a-cell-2022
- **Participants:** Cody Rhodes vs. Seth "Freakin" Rollins
- **Event & Date:** WWE Hell in a Cell — June 5, 2022 (Chicago, IL)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Hell in a Cell (the rubber match of their trilogy)
- **Result:** Cody Rhodes won — while wrestling with a completely torn pectoral muscle, his chest a gruesome purple-black bruise

- **MAT rating:** ★★★★★ — Courage as spectacle; unforgettable and five-star-rated.

## 48. The Inaugural Iron Survivor Challenge(s)
- **Slug:** iron-survivor-challenge-nxt-deadline-2022
- **Participants:** (Women's) Roxanne Perez, Cora Jade, Zoey Stark, Kiana James, Indi Hartwell; (Men's) Grayson Waller, Carmelo Hayes, JD McDonagh, Joe Gacy, Axiom
- **Event & Date:** WWE NXT Deadline — December 10, 2022 (Orlando, FL)
- **Promotion:** WWE NXT
- **Match type:** Iron Survivor Challenge (inaugural) — timed five-way; most falls in 25 minutes earns a title shot; penalty box for infractions
- **Result:** Roxanne Perez won the women's; Grayson Waller won the men's
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~3.75–4★; Cagematch ~7.5

Perez's win kicked off her rise as a franchise player. Included as a landmark of format innovation and the NXT 2.0/renewed era rather than pure workrate; the concept has since become an annual highlight.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★ — Inventive, fast-paced, and a genuine addition to the match-type canon.

## 49. Roman Reigns vs. Cody Rhodes (WrestleMania 39)
- **Slug:** roman-reigns-vs-cody-rhodes-wm39-2023
- **Participants:** Roman Reigns vs. Cody Rhodes
- **Event & Date:** WWE WrestleMania 39, Night 2 — April 2, 2023 (Inglewood, CA)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Singles — Undisputed WWE Universal Championship
- **Result:** Roman Reigns retained (after Bloodline interference), controversially denying Cody's "finish the story"

The decision to make fans wait a full year became one of WWE's most debated booking calls, and set up the two-night WrestleMania 40 climax. As a match, a strong, dramatic main event; as a story beat, a deliberate gut-punch.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★ — Excellent in-ring; its divisive finish is inseparable from its legacy.

## 50. Cody Rhodes vs. Roman Reigns ("Finish the Story")
- **Slug:** cody-rhodes-vs-roman-reigns-wm40-2024
- **Participants:** Cody Rhodes vs. Roman Reigns
- **Event & Date:** WWE WrestleMania XL, Night 2 — April 7, 2024 (Philadelphia, PA)
- **Promotion:** WWE
- **Match type:** Bloodline Rules — Undisputed WWE Universal Championship
- **Result:** Cody Rhodes won the Undisputed WWE Universal Championship, ending Roman Reigns' ~1,316-day reign — "finishing the story"
- **Critical rating:** Meltzer ~4.75★; Cagematch ~9.0
- **Why it matters:** The payoff of the modern era — a two-year saga culminating in an over-the-top "Bloodline Rules" spectacle featuring run-ins from Solo Sikoa, Jimmy Uso, then babyface saves by Seth Rollins, John Cena, and The Undertaker before Cody finally slew the champion. It ended the longest world title reign in four decades and delivered the cathartic moment WWE had teased since Cody's 2022 return. Divisive in its overbooking, unforgettable in its emotion.
- **Video:** Yes — WWE Network/Peacock, WWE YouTube.
- **MAT rating:** ★★★★½ — The definitive modern-WWE payoff; a genuine "moment" main event.
