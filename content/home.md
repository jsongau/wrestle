# MAT — The Ultimate Pro Wrestling Database & Fan Network

MAT (Match · Athlete · Timeline) is the definitive crawlable database of modern professional wrestling from 1997 to today. It profiles the greatest wrestlers, star-rates the most important matches across WWE, WCW, ECW, TNA/Impact and NXT, and maps the rivalries and real-life relationships — marriages, families, factions and backstage feuds — that connect every performer to the next.

## What is MAT?

MAT is a definitive, interlinked pro-wrestling encyclopedia and match-review network — think Sherdog or Tapology, but for professional wrestling. It covers the modern era (the 1997 Monday Night Wars onward) across five promotions: WWE (formerly WWF), WCW, ECW, TNA/Impact and WWE NXT. The name stands for Match · Athlete · Timeline. Its differentiator is the relationship graph: every wrestler links to rivals, tag partners, trainers, real-life spouses and family, and the matches that define them.

## What does MAT cover?

MAT covers 40+ wrestler profiles, 30+ star-rated matches, 15 landmark rivalries, and a real-life relationship web of 160+ links, spanning 29 years of wrestling history (1997–present). The five promotions in scope are WWE/WWF, WCW, ECW, TNA/Impact and WWE NXT. Coverage begins with the Monday Night Wars and runs through the Attitude Era, the Ruthless Aggression period, the TNA/ROH indie boom, and the NXT golden age and women's revolution.

## Is MAT free?

Yes. The core database is free forever. MAT Insider (also free to join) unlocks full star-ratings, the classic-match archive and the weekly "Rewind" newsletter. The paid Ringside tier ($6/month, illustrative) adds the premium archive, live watch-alongs, a members-only community and quarterly collectible drops. See /membership/ for details.

## How does MAT rate matches?

Every match gets a MAT rating out of five stars — an editorial score informed by critical consensus (Dave Meltzer's Wrestling Observer star ratings and Cagematch community scores) plus historical significance. MAT Insiders can add their own ratings. See the full approach at /methodology/.
