# How MAT Rates Matches (Methodology)

MAT rates every match on a five-star scale, from ★ to ★★★★★. The MAT rating is an editorial call by MAT's historian, informed by three inputs: critical consensus (Dave Meltzer's Wrestling Observer star ratings), community consensus (the Cagematch database), and historical significance. It is not a straight average of other sites' scores — it weighs in-ring quality against a match's lasting impact.

## What is the MAT five-star scale?

The MAT rating runs from one to five stars (out of 5), where ★★★★★ marks an all-time classic and lower scores reflect flaws in execution, pacing or booking. Ratings can use half-stars (for example, ★★★½ or ★★★★½). Across MAT's 30-match dataset, eleven matches earn a perfect ★★★★★, and the lowest rating is ★★★½ (Sting vs. Hogan, Starrcade 1997 — historically huge but undercut by a botched finish).

## What sources inform a MAT rating?

MAT draws on three reference points:

- Meltzer — the Wrestling Observer Newsletter star rating, historically out of 5 (some post-2018 ratings exceed 5). Meltzer's ratings are cited as reported; he has revised several over the years, so where consensus differs it is flagged.
- Cagematch — the community rating from the Cagematch database, expressed out of 10 (approximate where the live site was inaccessible).
- Historical weight — the match's significance to its promotion, era or the industry, independent of pure workrate.

## How does significance factor into the rating?

Significance can raise a rating above what workrate alone would justify, and a botched or muddled finish can lower it. For example, the nWo formation angle (Bash at the Beach 1996) is roughly a two-star match in the ring but earns a ★★★★½ MAT rating for being arguably the most influential angle of the Monday Night War. Conversely, Sting vs. Hogan at Starrcade 1997 had an 18-month build but a bungled finish, so it lands at ★★★½ despite its emotional stakes.

## Why do MAT ratings sometimes differ from Meltzer's?

Because MAT weighs historical impact alongside in-ring quality, and because Meltzer occasionally withheld a fifth star for reasons beyond match quality. The Undertaker vs. Shawn Michaels at WrestleMania XXV is the clearest case: Meltzer rated it 4.75★ (he later explained a dive scare cost it the fifth star), but critical and fan consensus — and MAT — treat it as a perfect ★★★★★ classic.

## Who awards MAT ratings, and can members add their own?

The baseline MAT rating is an editorial score set by MAT's historian. MAT Insiders (the free membership tier) can add their own star-ratings on top, so each match page can show both the editorial MAT rating and community input. This trust-and-authority approach — transparent sourcing plus a consistent scale — is why MAT publishes this methodology page.
