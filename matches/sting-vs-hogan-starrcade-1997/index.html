    Sting vs Hollywood Hulk Hogan

    WCW Starrcade — December 28, 1997 · MCI Center, Washington, D.C.

    ▶ Opens the official WWE match search. Verified embed IDs (YouTube & Bilibili for China) drop straight into this player.

    The Story

    Sting won the WCW World Heavyweight Championship — but only after a controversial referee count and a Bret Hart-ordered restart turned the payoff into a mess. Starrcade 1997 was the most anticipated match in company history, the climax of WCW's greatest slow-burn story, and its botched ending is still taught as the ultimate lesson in undoing a perfect build.

    The build was flawless. For roughly 18 months Sting abandoned his colourful surfer persona to become the silent, face-painted "Crow" — perched in the rafters, refusing to speak, stalking the nWo and never quite engaging Hollywood Hulk Hogan. By the time the two finally met, the anticipation was almost unbearable; the avenging hero was meant to conquer the man who had corrupted WCW.

    The finish sabotaged it. Referee Nick Patrick was supposed to deliver a visibly fast count on Sting to advance the storyline, but the count looked ordinary, leaving fans confused about whether Hogan had beaten Sting clean in the biggest match of the decade. Bret Hart, freshly arrived from the WWF, stormed in, restarted the bout, and Sting made Hogan submit to the Scorpion Deathlock. Sting got the belt, but the muddled sequence blunted the moment — a booking failure many historians mark as the start of WCW's decline.

    FAQ

    Who won Sting vs Hulk Hogan at Starrcade 1997?
    Sting won the WCW World Heavyweight Championship, but only after a controversial referee count and a restart ordered by Bret Hart made the finish infamous.
    Why was the Starrcade 1997 finish controversial?
    Referee Nick Patrick was supposed to deliver a fast count to further the story, but the count looked normal. Bret Hart intervened and restarted the match, muddying an ending that was meant to be a clean, triumphant payoff for Sting.
    What star rating did the match get?
    Dave Meltzer rated it around 2 to 2.5 stars — a celebrated build with botched execution. Wrestle Lore rates it 3.5 stars: historic and emotionally huge, dragged down by its self-inflicted finish.
