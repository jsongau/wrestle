    Terry Funk vs Sabu

    ECW Born to Be Wired — August 9, 1997 · Hammonton, NJ

    ▶ Opens the official WWE match search. Verified embed IDs (YouTube & Bilibili for China) drop straight into this player.

    The Story

    Terry Funk won the ECW World Heavyweight Championship in a match with no ropes — only strands of barbed wire ringing the ring. It is not a wrestling clinic and was never meant to be; it is the single most complete statement of what made ECW a cult phenomenon, and why the promotion's identity was built on courage bordering on lunacy.

    The premise was brutally simple. Strip out the ropes, replace them with barbed wire, and let two of the company's most fearless performers do the rest. Both men were repeatedly whipped into, tangled in, and dragged across the wire, and the crowd inside the tiny building reacted to every fresh gash as if it were a title change in itself.

    The enduring image is Sabu tearing open his own bicep on the wire, then having it taped shut at ringside so he could continue — a moment of genuine self-destruction that no choreography could fake. That a 53-year-old Terry Funk absorbed the same punishment and walked out champion only sharpened the legend. Decades later it remains the promotion's most-cited hardcore classic, referenced whenever anyone tries to explain the ECW ethos to the uninitiated.

    FAQ

    Who won Terry Funk vs Sabu at Born to Be Wired?
    Terry Funk won, capturing the ECW World Heavyweight Championship in the barbed-wire ropes match at age 53.
    What star rating did the barbed-wire match get?
    Dave Meltzer graded it around 3.75–4 stars as a spectacle. Wrestle Lore rates it 4 stars — not a technical bout, but the purest distillation of ECW's ultraviolent identity.
    Why is this match so infamous?
    Barbed wire replaced the ring ropes, both men became tangled and shredded in it, and Sabu tore open his bicep and taped it shut mid-match to keep going. It is ECW's most-cited hardcore classic.
