    Chris Benoit vs Triple H vs Shawn Michaels

    WWE WrestleMania XX — March 14, 2004 · Madison Square Garden, New York, NY

    ▶ Opens the official WWE match search. Verified embed IDs (YouTube & Bilibili for China) drop straight into this player.

    The Story

    Chris Benoit won the World Heavyweight Championship, submitting Triple H with the Crippler Crossface in the main event of WrestleMania XX at Madison Square Garden. It matters as the ultimate underdog-makes-good story of the era: a career mid-card technician finally reaching the mountaintop on the grandest stage.

    The three-way structure was worked as a genuine masterclass in pacing. Shawn Michaels brought the drama and the near-falls, Triple H the ruthless control segments, and Benoit the relentless technical fire, each combination of two men telling a different story before the third crashed back in. The MSG crowd, sensing something historic, invested fully in the smallest competitor's chase.

    The finish delivered catharsis: Benoit locking the Crossface on Triple H and refusing to let go until the champion tapped. The emotional coda — Benoit celebrating in the ring with best friend and fellow new champion Eddie Guerrero — became one of WWE's most beloved images. It should be noted that the match is now largely suppressed on official platforms and its legacy is deeply complicated by later events, but on the night, in the ring, it was an all-time WrestleMania main event.

    FAQ

    Who won Benoit vs Triple H vs Shawn Michaels at WrestleMania XX?
    Chris Benoit won the World Heavyweight Championship, forcing Triple H to tap out to the Crippler Crossface in the main event.
    What star rating did the WrestleMania XX main event get?
    Dave Meltzer awarded 4.75 stars; Cagematch community scores sit around 9.0/10. Wrestle Lore rates it a full 5 stars — an all-time WrestleMania main event.
    Why is this match hard to find officially?
    WWE has largely removed Chris Benoit's content from its official platforms following later events, so the match is rarely streamed or promoted. Its in-ring greatness and its legacy are uniquely complicated.
