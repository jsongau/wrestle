    WWE / WWF

    WWE (World Wrestling Entertainment, formerly the WWF) is the largest and most influential professional wrestling promotion in the world. It defined the modern era: its late-1990s Attitude Era boom — powered by Stone Cold Steve Austin, The Rock, Mick Foley and The Undertaker — won the Monday Night Wars over WCW, absorbed both WCW and ECW in 2001, and evolved through the Ruthless Aggression era into today's global product built around WrestleMania and stars like Roman Reigns and Cody Rhodes.

    A short history of WWE

    The company Vince McMahon expanded nationally in the 1980s rode Hulk Hogan's Hulkamania to the mainstream, then leaned on the technical excellence of Bret Hart and Shawn Michaels to bridge into the 1990s. The infamous 1997 Montreal Screwjob — Hart losing the title to Michaels against his wishes — closed one era and helped ignite the next.

    The Attitude Era (roughly 1997–2002) was WWE's commercial peak. An edgier, adult product built around the anti-authority "Austin 3:16" phenomenon, The Rock's electrifying mic work, Foley's hardcore sacrifices and the McMahon family villainy drew record audiences and beat WCW's Nitro in the ratings war. In 2001 the WWF purchased its rivals WCW and ECW; in 2002 a legal loss to the World Wildlife Fund forced the rebrand from WWF to WWE.

    The Ruthless Aggression era that followed elevated Brock Lesnar, John Cena, Randy Orton, Batista and Edge, while Triple H transitioned from top heel to executive. The 2010s brought the grassroots "Yes Movement" behind Daniel Bryan, the CM Punk "pipe bomb," The Shield, and a women's revolution that saw Becky Lynch and Charlotte Flair main-event WrestleMania. Today the promotion is anchored by The Bloodline saga and Cody Rhodes "finishing the story" against Roman Reigns at WrestleMania 40.

    Frequently Asked

    What is WWE?
    WWE (World Wrestling Entertainment, formerly the WWF / World Wrestling Federation) is the largest professional wrestling promotion in the world. It defined the modern era through its Attitude Era boom of the late 1990s, its Ruthless Aggression period, and today's global product headlined by WrestleMania and stars like Roman Reigns, Cody Rhodes and Becky Lynch.
    Why did the WWF change its name to WWE?
    In 2002 the company lost a legal dispute with the World Wildlife Fund over the "WWF" initials and rebranded as WWE (World Wrestling Entertainment). Footage and history from before 2002 is still commonly referred to as the WWF era.
    What was the Attitude Era?
    The Attitude Era (roughly 1997–2002) was WWE's most commercially explosive period, built on edgier, adult-oriented storytelling and stars like Stone Cold Steve Austin, The Rock, Mick Foley and The Undertaker. It powered the WWF past WCW in the Monday Night Wars.
