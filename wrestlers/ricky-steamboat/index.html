    01

    Overview

    Ricky Steamboat is the babyface that wrestling's best heels measured themselves against. He won the NWA World Heavyweight Championship from Ric Flair at Chi-Town Rumble on February 20, 1989, and his WrestleMania III win over Randy Savage on March 29, 1987 ranks among the most praised matches in WrestleMania history. 2Match of the Year awards from both PWI and the Observer, 1987 vs. Savage and 1989 vs. FlairBoth feuds won Match of the Year from Pro Wrestling Illustrated and the Wrestling Observer: 1987 for Savage, 1989 for Flair. Around those peaks sit four United States title reigns, four Television title reigns, eight World tag title reigns, a WWF Intercontinental Championship and a 2009 WWE Hall of Fame induction, delivered by Flair.

    Setting one thing straight

    Three things repeated about him are wrong, and this page used to repeat them. He entered the WWE Hall of Fame in 2009, not 2010; Flair inducted him the night before WrestleMania 25 in Houston. The WrestleMania III match was never a Dave Meltzer five-star match; it does not appear on Meltzer's list. The 1989 Flair matches do, all three televised ones at five stars, plus an untelevised Landover house show on March 18, 1989 that Meltzer rated six stars and called unofficial. And he did not win at Backlash 2009. Chris Jericho made him tap to the Walls of Jericho.

    He was born Richard Henry Blood on February 28, 1953, in West Point, New York, to a Japanese American mother and a white father. He was a Florida state amateur champion, trained under Verne Gagne and The Iron Sheik, and debuted in the AWA in 1976 as Rick Blood. Eddie Graham renamed him in Florida after Sammy Steamboat, whom he resembled. The two were not related. 1977his first title: the Mid-Atlantic TV belt, from Ric Flair, in a Raleigh TV studioJim Crockett's Mid-Atlantic office hired him in 1977 on Wahoo McDaniel's word. On June 15, 1977, in a TV studio in Raleigh, he pinned Ric Flair for the Mid-Atlantic Television title. That started the longest-running rivalry of both men's careers. Through 1983 he traded the United States title with Flair and won World tag titles with Paul Jones and Jay Youngblood, the last of them at the first Starrcade.

    The WWF signed him in 1985 and dressed him as The Dragon. Savage crushed his throat with the ring bell in late 1986, and the payoff at the Pontiac Silverdome went 14:35 and ended with a small package. He left the WWF after WrestleMania IV, came back to the NWA in January 1989, and beat Flair for the world title. 55:49the length of the two-out-of-three-falls Clash VI match in New Orleans, April 2, 1989He kept it at Clash of the Champions VI on April 2 in a two-out-of-three-falls match that ran 55:49, then lost it at WrestleWar on May 7 when his knee gave out. His WCW run from 1991 to 1994 added the WarGames match, an Iron Man win over Rick Rude, and a long series with "Stunning" Steve Austin. The last match of it, at Clash of the Champions XXVIII on August 24, 1994, won him the US title and hurt his back. Eric Bischoff fired him by FedEx that September.

    Since then he has refereed for TNA, worked a 2004 program opposite CM Punk in Ring of Honor, produced for WWE from 2005, and trained wrestlers in WWE developmental. He wrestled Jericho twice in 2009. On November 27, 2022, at 69, he teamed with FTR in Raleigh and won his first match in 12 years. His later work has been AEW cameos: guest enforcer for CM Punk vs. Ricky Starks in 2023, and a November 12, 2025 segment where FTR attacked him at his own request for "firm" offense. In February 2026 a Ric Flair post set off a false death scare. PWInsider reported him "100% OK," and his wife said there had been no emergency. No match is booked.

    05

    Before WWE

    Nine years in the territories before the WWF ever called.

    AWA · 1976

    Rick Blood

    Trained by Verne Gagne and The Iron Sheik

    Debuted in Minneapolis as a babyface under his own name. He left in May 1976, after a two-ring battle royal won by Mad Dog Vachon and Larry Hennig.

    Florida · 1976

    Ricky Steamboat is born

    Championship Wrestling from Florida

    Eddie Graham renamed him for his likeness to Sammy Steamboat and pitched him as Sammy's nephew. The name stuck for 50 years. The family tie never existed.

    Mid-Atlantic · 1977–79

    Steamboat vs. Flair, act one

    Ric Flair

    A June 15, 1977 TV title win over Flair in a Raleigh studio made him a star inside a year. They traded the US title into 1979, including a December 1978 cage match in Greensboro where Steamboat's hair was on the line.

    Mid-Atlantic · 1978–79

    Steamboat & Paul Jones

    Paul Jones

    World tag champions in April 1978 and PWI's Tag Team of the Year. Jones turned on him at a Charlotte battle royal on December 3, 1978, and the partners became a feud.

    Mid-Atlantic · 1979–83

    Steamboat & Jay Youngblood

    Jay Youngblood

    Five World tag title reigns and the Observer's 1983 Tag Team of the Year. The last reign began at the first Starrcade, over Jack and Jerry Brisco, and ended on Christmas Day 1983 when Steamboat announced his retirement. He was back by April 1984.

    06

    Personas

    One babyface for 18 years, in four costumes.

    RBAWA · 1976

    Rick Blood

    His real surname, a good amateur background, and no gimmick at all.

    RSMid-Atlantic · 1976–85

    Ricky Steamboat

    The territory star with the chops, the arm drags and the crossbody. Billed from Honolulu and booked as the clean opposite of Flair.

    DRWWF · 1985–88

    The Dragon

    Keikogi, long tights and a kung-fu frame, including a 1985 kung-fu challenge win over Mr. Fuji. The look sold him to a national audience; the matches with Jake Roberts and Randy Savage kept him there.

    FBWWF · 1991

    The Dragon, fire-breather

    Billed with no surname, introduced in February 1991 vignettes breathing fire. It lasted until late 1991, when he quit before Survivor Series and went to WCW.

    LG2005–present

    The legend

    WWE producer, developmental trainer, 2009 Hall of Famer, and since 2022 a guest who keeps getting jumped on AEW television, at his own suggestion.

    07

    Career Timeline

    From the AWA in 1976 to an AEW segment in 2025, with the best work in the middle.

    1976

    Debut as Rick Blood

    Starts in the AWA, then moves to Florida in May, where Eddie Graham names him Ricky Steamboat.

    1977

    First title, from Flair

    Joins Jim Crockett Promotions and pins Ric Flair for the Mid-Atlantic TV title on June 15. PWI names him Rookie of the Year.

    1978

    Tag gold and a hair match

    Wins the World tag titles with Paul Jones in April, loses Jones to a heel turn in December, and regains the US title from Flair in a Greensboro cage with his hair at stake.

    1983

    Starrcade and a short retirement

    Wins the World tag titles with Jay Youngblood at the first Starrcade on November 24, then announces his retirement in December. He is back the next spring.

    1985

    WWF and WrestleMania

    Signs with the WWF as The Dragon and beats Matt Borne at the first WrestleMania on March 31.

    1986

    Roberts and the bell

    Jake Roberts DDTs him on the concrete; Steamboat wins their Snake Pit match in front of 61,470 paid in Toronto. Randy Savage then crushes his throat with the ring bell.

    1987

    WrestleMania III

    Beats Savage for the Intercontinental title on March 29, the first IC title change at a WrestleMania. Drops it to The Honky Tonk Man in June.

    1988

    Leaves the WWF

    Loses to Greg Valentine in the WrestleMania IV tournament on March 27 and departs.

    1989

    World champion

    Returns to the NWA in January, beats Flair at Chi-Town Rumble on February 20, keeps the belt at Clash VI on April 2, and loses it at WrestleWar on May 7. Lex Luger turns on him that summer. He leaves over his contract at year's end.

    1990

    Japan and the independents

    Foot surgery, then work for the NAWA/SAPW group in the Carolinas and a New Japan tour in September and October.

    1991

    The Dragon, then WCW

    Back in the WWF as a fire-breathing Dragon from March. He quits before Survivor Series and debuts in WCW on November 19 as Dustin Rhodes's surprise partner, winning the tag titles that night.

    1992

    WarGames and the Iron Man

    Fights the Dangerous Alliance all year: the five-star WarGames on May 17 and a 4-3 Iron Man win over Rick Rude on June 20. Takes the TV title from Steve Austin and the tag titles with Shane Douglas.

    1993

    The Blonds and Regal

    Loses the tag titles to Austin and Brian Pillman, wins the TV title from Paul Orndorff in August, and loses it to Lord Steven Regal in September.

    1994

    Last WCW run

    Double pin with Flair at Spring Stampede on April 17. Wins the US title from Austin on August 24 but hurts his back, gives up the belt in September, and is fired by Eric Bischoff by FedEx.

    2004

    Ring of Honor

    After refereeing for TNA, works a 2004 program in ROH against CM Punk, then Mick Foley.

    2005

    WWE producer

    Rejoins WWE as a producer and is reintroduced as a Legend on Raw in October.

    2009

    Hall of Fame and two Jericho matches

    Inducted by Ric Flair on April 4 in Houston. Wrestles Chris Jericho at WrestleMania 25 the next night and again at Backlash on April 26. PWI names him Most Inspirational.

    2010

    The Nexus attack

    Teams with his son Richie in FCW on June 18. Ten days later The Nexus attack him on Raw while he promotes his DVD; in 2024 he said the injury kept him in hospital three weeks.

    2014

    Leaves WWE

    Released on July 19 after years as a developmental trainer and ambassador.

    2022

    Return of the Dragon

    Teams with FTR on November 27 in Raleigh to beat Nick Aldis, Jay Lethal and Brock Anderson. All three tap to figure-fours. He is 69.

    2023

    AEW cameos

    Guest enforcer for CM Punk vs. Ricky Starks on August 5; Starks beats him with his own belt, a spot Steamboat says he pitched. Turns down a backstage producer job because of the travel.

    2025

    FTR again

    Appears at AEW Blood & Guts on November 12 to tell a Flair story and is attacked by FTR, his 2022 partners.

    2026

    A false alarm

    A February post by Ric Flair is read as a eulogy. PWInsider reports him fine and his wife confirms there was no emergency. No match is booked as of November 9.

    08

    Rivalries

    Who he went to war with, in order of how much it mattered.

    Ric Flair

    Seventeen years of it. A 1977 TV title in a Raleigh studio, US title trades through 1979, and the 1989 series: Chi-Town Rumble, the six-star Landover house show, Clash VI and WrestleWar. Then a 1994 double pin at Spring Stampede. Flair inducted him into the Hall of Fame in 2009 and walked him to the ring at WrestleMania 25.

    Randy Savage

    Savage dropped the ring bell on his throat from the top rope in late 1986. The six-month build ended at WrestleMania III on March 29, 1987, with George Steele stopping Savage's bell and Steamboat winning the title with a small package. It was 1987 Match of the Year for PWI and the Observer.

    Steve Austin

    Steamboat's main WCW opponent, back when Austin was "Stunning" Steve Austin. Steamboat took his TV title in 1992, lost the tag titles to Austin's Hollywood Blonds in 1993, and swapped wins with him over the US title in 1994. The last of those matches ended Steamboat's career in WCW.

    Jake Roberts

    The 1986 program built on a DDT onto the concrete that knocked Steamboat out for real. Steamboat won the Snake Pit match at The Big Event in Toronto and the Saturday Night's Main Event rematch in October.

    Rick Rude

    The face of the Dangerous Alliance in 1992. Rude kept the US title at SuperBrawl II with a ninja-disguised Paul E. Dangerously's help; Steamboat beat him 4-3 in a 30-minute Iron Man match at Beach Blast.

    Chris Jericho

    The 2009 coda. At WrestleMania 25 Steamboat outlasted Jimmy Snuka and Roddy Piper in a handicap match before Jericho's Codebreaker; at Backlash he tapped to the Walls. PWI named him its Most Inspirational Wrestler that year, at 56.

    Lex Luger

    Luger turned on him at Clash of the Champions VII in June 1989. Their Great American Bash match on July 23 ended with Steamboat disqualified for using the chair Luger brought in.

    Frequently Asked

    Is Ricky Steamboat still alive, and is he still wrestling?

    He is alive, and he is not wrestling. A February 2026 Ric Flair post set off a false death scare; PWInsider reported him “100% OK” and his wife said there had been no emergency. His last match was November 27, 2022, a six-man tag win with FTR in Raleigh at 69. His most recent TV appearance found is AEW Blood & Guts on November 12, 2025. As of November 9, 2026, nothing is booked.

    Did Ricky Steamboat win the NWA World Heavyweight Championship?

    Yes, once. He beat Ric Flair at Chi-Town Rumble in Chicago on February 20, 1989 with a small package, kept the title against Flair at Clash of the Champions VI on April 2, and lost it back at WrestleWar on May 7. The reign lasted 76 days.

    What is the Steamboat and Flair trilogy?

    Three televised 1989 NWA World title matches: Chi-Town Rumble (February 20, Steamboat wins the belt), Clash of the Champions VI (April 2, Steamboat keeps it two falls to one in 55:49), and WrestleWar (May 7, Flair wins it back). Dave Meltzer rated all three five stars. An untelevised March 18 match in Landover, Maryland got six. The two had already been feuding since 1977.

    Was Steamboat vs. Savage at WrestleMania III a five-star match?

    Not by Dave Meltzer. The match is not on Meltzer's list of five-star matches, though it is often described that way. What it did win is 1987 Match of the Year from both Pro Wrestling Illustrated and the Wrestling Observer. Steamboat won the Intercontinental title with a small package at 14:35, with George Steele in his corner.

    When was Ricky Steamboat inducted into the WWE Hall of Fame?

    In 2009, not 2010. Ric Flair inducted him at the Toyota Center in Houston the night before WrestleMania 25. In his speech he pointed at Mickey Rourke and said, “Tonight, this is my Oscar.”

    Why did Ricky Steamboat leave WCW in 1994?

    He hurt his back beating Steve Austin for the US title at Clash of the Champions XXVIII on August 24, 1994. He could not defend it at Fall Brawl on September 18, so WCW gave the title to Austin. That September, while he was still injured, Eric Bischoff fired him by FedEx.

    Did Ricky Steamboat wrestle after 1994?

    Rarely. Four matches stand out. He wrestled Chris Jericho at WrestleMania 25 on April 5, 2009 (a handicap match with Jimmy Snuka and Roddy Piper) and at Backlash on April 26, 2009, losing both. He teamed with his son Richie in FCW on June 18, 2010. Then, after 12 years away, he won with FTR at Big Time Wrestling on November 27, 2022.

    Is Ricky Steamboat related to Sammy Steamboat?

    No. His real name is Richard Henry Blood. Florida promoter Eddie Graham gave him the Steamboat name in 1976 because he looked like the Hawaiian wrestler Sammy Steamboat, and billed him as Sammy's nephew. His real family in wrestling is his younger brother Vic Steamboat and his son Richie.

    How is Ricky Steamboat connected to Steve Austin?

    He was the veteran Austin learned against in WCW. Steamboat took Austin's TV title on September 2, 1992. Austin and Brian Pillman's Hollywood Blonds took the tag titles from Steamboat and Shane Douglas in March 1993. In 1994 Austin beat him at Bash at the Beach and Steamboat beat Austin for the US title at Clash XXVIII. Steamboat mentioned that last match in his 2009 Hall of Fame speech.
