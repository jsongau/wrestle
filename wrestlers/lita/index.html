    01

    Overview

    Lita got into wrestling by buying a ticket to Mexico City because she liked how lucha libre looked, and everything about the career that followed kept that shape: self-taught momentum over polish, a moonsault in an era of headlocks, thong-strap jeans in an era of evening gowns. 4WWE Women’s Championship reigns between 2000 and 2006 — and a tag title seventeen years laterThe concrete record: four WWF/WWE Women's Championship reigns between 2000 and 2006, the first women's match to main event Raw, the first women's steel cage match in Raw history, the 2014 Hall of Fame, and — the late plot twist — a WWE Women's Tag Team Championship won with Becky Lynch in 2023, seventeen years after her last title and twenty-three years after her first.

    Setting one thing straight

    One thing needs setting straight about how her 2023 run ended, because even the sources half-garble it. Her last match is the WrestleMania 39 six-woman tag on April 1, 2023 — the win with Becky Lynch and Trish Stratus over Damage CTRL — and not the April 10 Raw where the tag titles were lost. She was not in that match: she was taken out backstage beforehand in the angle that seeded Trish Stratus's heel turn, Lynch defended with a substitute, and the belts went to Liv Morgan and Raquel Rodriguez. 411Mania states it plainly — she has not competed since WrestleMania 39 and was written off television via the attack. So the tag reign's dates (February 27 to April 10, 2023) outrun her own last bell by nine days, a distinction almost every capsule bio flattens. One further note on this page's own sourcing: an automated read of Wikipedia returned an impossible date for her fourth title win; the verified Cyber Sunday date of November 5, 2006 is used instead.

    She was born Amy Christine Dumas on April 14, 1975 in Fort Lauderdale, found lucha libre on television, and went to Mexico in the late 1990s to learn it — debuting in 1998 and passing through ECW in 1999 before the WWF signed her. 2000the year of Team Xtreme, the Stephanie McMahon title win, and the moonsault that minted a generation of fansPaired with Matt and Jeff Hardy as Team Xtreme, she became the rare valet who outdrew her team: the August 21, 2000 Raw title win over Stephanie McMahon-Helmsley, with The Rock refereeing, made her champion inside six months of her television debut. The years that followed were defined by the Trish Stratus rivalry — ringside brawls in 2000, the first women's Raw main event on December 6, 2004, which Lita won for her second title, and the Unforgiven 2006 retirement match she lost — and by a broken neck in 2002, suffered filming a television stunt, that cost her a year and a half at her peak.

    The 2005–06 heel run — the real-life Edge storyline turned on-screen “Rated-R” era — made her the most jeered woman in the company, and her November 26, 2006 retirement match at Survivor Series was played as a heel's send-off rather than a farewell. Wrestling spent the next two decades correcting that. 47her age winning the WWE Women’s Tag Team Championship with Becky Lynch, February 27, 2023The 2014 Hall of Fame induction — by Trish Stratus — the first women's Royal Rumble in 2018, the Evolution tag win, the 2022 Elimination Chamber title challenge against Becky Lynch that ended in a standing ovation, and the 2023 tag championship all recast her as what she had actually been all along: the template for every high-flying, alternative-styled woman who came after. As of August 31, 2026 she is a legend at large — on Busted Open in April she named Iyo Sky as the one opponent she would come back for, and no such match is booked.

    05

    Factions

    The team that defined an era of television, the storyline that defined a tabloid one, and the late alliance that got her gold again.

    WWF/WWE · 2000–05

    Team Xtreme

    Matt Hardy, Jeff Hardy, Lita

    The Hardy Boyz plus the valet who wrestled like a third Hardy. The trio was arguably the most popular act of the early-2000s youth audience, and Lita's moonsaults off their ladders are the reason a generation of women cite her as the way in. WWE.com dates the partnership across roughly five years.

    WWE · 2005–06

    Edge & Lita

    Edge, Lita

    The "Rated-R" pairing that grew out of a real-life story WWE chose to put on television, making her the company's most-booed performer and, in commercial terms, one of its most effective heels. It produced her 2006 title reigns, the live "celebration" segment that still gets cited in every retrospective on the era's excesses, and the heel-exit framing of her retirement.

    WWE · 2018–23

    Team Bestie / the Becky alliance

    Lita, Trish Stratus; later Becky Lynch

    The legends' wing: Evolution 2018 with Trish, then the 2023 Becky Lynch alliance that delivered the tag championship and the WrestleMania 39 six-woman win over Damage CTRL — before the backstage-attack angle wrote her out and turned Trish heel. Her last on-screen chapter to date.

    06

    Personas

    One name from the WWF debut onward — the eras are marked by who she stood next to.

    ANMexico & ECW · 1998–99

    Angelica / Miss Congeniality

    The self-financed apprenticeship: to Mexico as Angelica after falling for lucha libre on TV, then ECW as Miss Congeniality — trained along the way by names including Dory Funk Jr. and Ricky Santana. The high-risk style was set before WWF ever saw her.

    TXWWF/WWE · 2000–04

    The Team Xtreme original

    Cargo pants, thong straps, the moonsault. Champion within six months of her TV debut, and half of the Trish rivalry that carried the division. A broken neck in 2002 — from a television stunt, not a match — took a year and a half out of the peak.

    RRWWE · 2005–06

    The Rated-R era

    The heel run alongside Edge, engineered out of real-life tabloid material and played to the loudest boos in the company. Two more title reigns, and a retirement at Survivor Series 2006 staged as a villain's ejection — the send-off the following two decades kept apologising for.

    HF2014–present

    The Hall of Famer

    Inducted in 2014 by Trish Stratus, and since then the division's favorite returning legend: the first women's Rumble, Evolution, the 2022 ovation match with Becky Lynch, tag gold at 47 in 2023 — and, in April 2026, a public offer to lace up once more if the opponent is Iyo Sky.

    07

    Career Timeline

    A one-way ticket to Mexico City to the Hall of Fame, with a broken neck and a punk band in the middle.

    1998

    Debuts in Mexico

    Self-financed training trips to Mexico after discovering lucha libre; debuts as Angelica, with ECW appearances following in 1999.

    2000

    Team Xtreme, and a title in six months

    Debuts with the WWF in February, pairs with the Hardy Boyz, and beats Stephanie McMahon-Helmsley for the Women's Championship on the August 21 Raw with The Rock as referee.

    2002

    The broken neck

    Fractures her neck filming a stunt for the TV series Dark Angel; out roughly a year and a half at the height of her popularity.

    2003

    The cage first

    Returns and wrestles Victoria in the first women's steel cage match in Raw history on November 24.

    2004

    The Raw main event

    Beats Trish Stratus on December 6 in the first women's match to main event Raw, winning her second Women's Championship.

    2006

    Two titles, and a heel's goodbye

    Wins the title in August and again at Cyber Sunday in November amid the Rated-R storyline, then retires at Survivor Series on November 26, dropping the belt to Mickie James. The Luchagors' debut album lands the following September.

    2014

    Hall of Fame

    Inducted by Trish Stratus — the rivalry formally recast as a partnership.

    2018

    The Rumble and Evolution

    Enters the first women's Royal Rumble on January 28, and wins the Evolution tag alongside Trish on October 28.

    2022

    The ovation match

    A surprise Rumble entry, then a Raw Women's Championship challenge to Becky Lynch at Elimination Chamber on February 19 — a loss received with a standing ovation.

    2023

    Champion again at 47

    Wins the Women's Tag Team Championship with Becky Lynch on February 27 and the WrestleMania 39 six-woman on April 1 — her last match — before the backstage-attack angle writes her out on April 10.

    2026

    Legend at large

    Talks legacy on the No Holds Barred podcast in February; names Iyo Sky as her dream comeback opponent on Busted Open in April; booked for Evansville's Raptor Con in September. No match is scheduled.

    08

    Rivalries

    Who she went to war with.

    Trish Stratus

    The rivalry that built the division's ceiling: 2000 valet brawls, the December 6, 2004 Raw main event Lita won, the New Year's Revolution rematch where her knee gave out, and Trish's 2006 retirement match in Toronto, which Lita lost. Then the reversal — Trish inducted her into the Hall of Fame in 2014, they won Evolution as partners, and the 2023 angle that ended Lita's run was built on Trish turning heel. Twenty-six years, and the story still is not over.

    Mickie James

    The opponent on both ends of her final act: Lita beat James for the title twice in 2006 — August, and Cyber Sunday in November — and James took the belt back at Survivor Series in Lita's retirement match, the crowd gleefully waving her out. They met once more on opposite sides at Evolution 2018.

    Victoria

    The workhorse rivalry of the comeback-from-the-broken-neck era, producing the first women's steel cage match in Raw history on November 24, 2003 — which Victoria won, a detail this page keeps because the milestone mattered more than the result.

    Becky Lynch

    Idol and inheritor: Lynch has been explicit that Lita is a foundational influence, and their 2022 Elimination Chamber title match doubled as a torch-passing ceremony with an ovation for the loser. A year later they were tag champions together. If the Iyo Sky comeback ever happens, it will be because this alliance proved she could still go.

    Frequently Asked

    Is Lita still wrestling, and will she return?

    She has not wrestled since the WrestleMania 39 six-woman tag on April 1, 2023, but she has publicly left the door open: on the Busted Open podcast in April 2026 — appearing alongside Trish Stratus and Kaitlyn — she said she is ready for a comeback and named Iyo Sky as the opponent, a singles match the two have never had despite crossing paths in the 2023 tag division. As of August 31, 2026 no match is scheduled; her calendar is conventions and podcasts, including Evansville's Raptor Con on September 12–13.

    What was Lita's last match — and was it the tag title loss?

    No, and the distinction is worth keeping. Her last match is the WrestleMania 39 Night 1 six-woman tag on April 1, 2023, won with Becky Lynch and Trish Stratus over Damage CTRL. The tag titles she held with Lynch were lost on the April 10, 2023 Raw to Liv Morgan and Raquel Rodriguez — a match Lita was not in, having been taken out backstage in the angle that set up Trish Stratus's heel turn. So her reign outlasted her last bell by nine days, and she left television as a champion who never lost the belts in the ring.

    How many championships did Lita win?

    Five in WWE: four WWF/WWE Women's Championship reigns — 2000 (from Stephanie McMahon-Helmsley, with The Rock refereeing), 2004 (from Trish Stratus in the first women's Raw main event), and twice from Mickie James in 2006 — plus the WWE Women's Tag Team Championship with Becky Lynch in 2023, won at 47, seventeen years after her previous title. Her Mexico and ECW years produced no documented championships in the sources used for this page.

    Why did Lita retire in 2006?

    She chose to leave — burnout with the character's direction and life on the road, in the middle of the Rated-R storyline that had turned real-life tabloid material into television and made her the most jeered performer in the company. WWE booked the exit as a heel's comeuppance: she dropped the title to Mickie James at Survivor Series on November 26, 2006 and was mocked out of the arena. The two decades since — the Hall of Fame, Evolution, the 2022 ovation, the 2023 title — have functioned as the farewell the original never was.
