    Biography

    Randy "Macho Man" Savage is one of the most complete performers in wrestling history. Equally effective as a face or heel, Savage combined extraordinary athleticism (he was a semi-professional baseball prospect), magnetic charisma, and an in-ring storytelling ability that few contemporaries could match.

    Savage arrived in WWF in 1985 and immediately stood apart. His feud with Ricky Steamboat — culminating at WrestleMania III in 1987 — produced what many consider the greatest match in WWF history to that point: a flawless piece of ring psychology that proved WWF could produce not just spectacle, but actual match quality.

    His relationship with Miss Elizabeth was one of wrestling's great storylines. The protective, domineering Savage and the gentle Elizabeth created genuine emotional investment, payingoff spectacularly when Elizabeth threw in the towel to save Savage at WrestleMania VII and they reunited in a tear-jerking segment that remains one of wrestling's most emotionally resonant moments.

    After WWF, Savage had a productive WCW run through the nWo era, but his peak was undeniably the WWF years 1985–1994. He was inducted into the WWE Hall of Fame posthumously in 2015 — a recognition that came far too late given his enormous contribution to the product.

    Finishing Moves

    • Flying Elbow Drop — The definitive top-rope elbow drop — Savage's height, timing, and follow-through made this the most theatrical elbow in the business.
    • Axe Handle Smash — Leaping double-axe handle from the top rope or the apron — used extensively as a mid-match weapon.

    Career Timeline

    1. 1973

      Pro debut

      Begins wrestling after a baseball career; trained by his father Angelo Poffo in the family's ICW promotion.

    2. 1985

      WWF debut

      Arrives in WWF with Miss Elizabeth — immediately the most compelling character on the roster.

    3. 1986

      WWF Intercontinental Champion

      Wins the IC title — begins a legendary 14-month reign that elevates the belt to near-world-title prestige.

    4. 1987

      WrestleMania III vs. Steamboat

      Produces what many call the greatest WWF match to date — an IC title match that proved WWF could do real wrestling.

    5. 1988

      WWF Champion

      Wins the WWF title at WrestleMania IV's tournament — the Mega Powers are formed with Hulk Hogan.

    6. 1989

      Mega Powers Explode

      Turns on Hogan in one of WWF's great heel turns — jealousy over Elizabeth becomes a monster feud.

    7. 1991

      WrestleMania VII reunion

      Loses to Ultimate Warrior, Elizabeth saves him — their reunion in the ring is one of wrestling's most emotional moments.

    8. 1992

      Second WWF title reign

      Wins vacant WWF title in a Battle Royal — a somewhat underplayed second reign before departing for WCW.

    9. 1995

      WCW Champion

      Brief WCW Championship reign — Savage remains a credible main eventer into his mid-40s.

    10. 2015

      Posthumous Hall of Fame

      Inducted into the WWE Hall of Fame four years after his death — a bittersweet but fitting recognition.

    FAQ

    What happened to Randy Savage?

    Randy Savage passed away on May 20, 2011, following a heart attack while driving in Seminole, Florida. He was 58 years old.

    Is Randy Savage in the WWE Hall of Fame?

    Yes — Savage was inducted posthumously in 2015, four years after his death, with his brother Lanny Poffo accepting on his behalf.

    What was Randy Savage's best match?

    Widely considered to be his WWF Intercontinental Championship match against Ricky Steamboat at WrestleMania III (March 29, 1987) — a five-star classic with 27 near-falls.

    Signature Matches

    ★★★★★

    Randy Savage vs. Ricky Steamboat

    WrestleMania III — Mar 29, 1987

    The greatest WWF match to that point — 14 minutes of flawless ring psychology, 27 near-falls, and a finish that had 93,173 fans on their feet. The blueprint for WWF matches for the next decade.

    ★★★★

    Randy Savage vs. Ted DiBiase

    WrestleMania IV — Mar 27, 1988

    Tournament final for the vacant WWF title — Savage wins with Elizabeth calling Hogan ringside. The Mega Powers are born in MSG.

    ★★★★

    Randy Savage vs. Ultimate Warrior

    WrestleMania VII — Mar 24, 1991

    Career vs. Career — Warrior wins but the post-match Elizabeth reunion transcends the match itself. A moment that defines WWF's emotional storytelling.
