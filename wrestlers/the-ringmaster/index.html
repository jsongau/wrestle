The Ringmaster

The Ringmaster was Steve Austin's first WWF gimmick — a technically gifted but blandly packaged heel managed by Ted DiBiase, holding the Million Dollar Championship. It didn't click. Austin shed the name in early 1996, became "Stone Cold," and after winning the 1996 King of the Ring delivered the "Austin 3:16" promo that changed his career and the industry.

The gimmick that had to fail first

When Austin arrived in the WWF in late 1995, creative handed him The Ringmaster — a corporate-backed technician managed by Ted DiBiase, who awarded him the Million Dollar Championship. The wrestling was fine; the character was forgettable, and Austin knew it.

In early 1996 he pushed to rebrand. The story goes that the "Stone Cold" nickname came from a throwaway line about cold tea — and it stuck. At the 1996 King of the Ring, newly minted as Stone Cold, he beat Jake Roberts in the final and cut the "Austin 3:16" promo: "Austin 3:16 says I just whipped your ass." It became the best-selling t-shirt in company history and the launchpad for the Attitude Era. The Ringmaster was the necessary false start.

What came next

Within a year Austin was the hottest act in wrestling, feuding with Bret Hart and then Mr. McMahon. See the complete record — WWF, WCW and ECW — on the main Stone Cold profile.
