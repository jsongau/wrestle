    01

    Overview

    Ric Flair is the most decorated world champion in the history of the business and its most complete performer over distance: a limousine-riding, jet-flying heel who could wrestle an hour every night of the week, in a different town, against a different local hero, and send the crowd home furious. Born February 25, 1949 — adopted as an infant in Memphis, an episode tied to the Tennessee Children's Home Society scandal — and raised in Minnesota, he debuted on December 10, 1972 in Rice Lake, Wisconsin, trained by Verne Gagne. Three years in, on October 4, 1975, a plane crash broke his back in three places; doctors told him at 26 that he was done, and he was wrestling again within months, having rebuilt himself from a 300-pound brawler into the figure-four-and-chops stylist the “Nature Boy” name required. Everything after — the robes, the “Woooo!”, the sixty-minute broadways — came out of that reinvention.

    Setting one thing straight

    Now the number. WWE recognizes Flair as a 16-time world champion; Flair himself claims 21, which is also the figure most sources land on; counts across the literature run as high as 25, depending on how you treat title changes the NWA later struck from the record and short reigns overseas. This page publishes the recognized 16 — commonly broken out as eight NWA, six WCW and two WWF reigns — and states the dispute rather than resolving it, because the sources genuinely disagree and the man himself is on the higher side. What no count disputes: the first reign began September 17, 1981 in Kansas City against Dusty Rhodes, with Lou Thesz refereeing, and for the following decade he was the touring NWA champion against whom every territory measured its best.

    The résumé peaks are specific. Starrcade 1983, November 24: he took the title from Harley Race in a steel cage, the match the first Starrcade was built around. The 1989 trilogy with Ricky Steamboat — Chi-Town Rumble on February 20, the 55-minute best-of-three at Clash VI on April 2, the regain at WrestleWar on May 7 — is the most acclaimed series of matches in American wrestling history, and he followed it immediately with the Terry Funk I Quit match at Clash IX on November 15. 60minutes in the 1992 Royal Rumble, entered at No. 3, to win the vacant WWF ChampionshipFired by WCW in 1991, he walked into the WWF with the big gold belt as “the Real World's Champion” and won the vacant WWF Championship in the 1992 Royal Rumble on January 19 — entering third and surviving the hour, still the definitive Rumble performance. He traded that title with Randy Savage in 1992, went home to WCW, lost the unification match to Hulk Hogan at Bash at the Beach on July 17, 1994, and kept headlining, on and off, into his fifties.

    He has retired twice, and only the second one held. WWE gave him the send-off at WrestleMania XXIV on March 30, 2008 — a career-threatening match Shawn Michaels ended with “I'm sorry, I love you” before the superkick — followed by the only Hall of Fame induction ever staged for an active competitor's farewell. It lasted eighteen months; he wrestled on for TNA into 2011. The real ending was July 31, 2022 in Nashville: Ric Flair's Last Match, a Starrcast event run under the revived Jim Crockett Promotions banner, where he and his son-in-law Andrade El Idolo beat Jeff Jarrett and Jay Lethal — Flair, 73, taking the pin-securing figure-four after brass knuckles found their way to him. Since then he has been a legends-deal presence — AEW signed him in that role in 2023, and he seconded Sting's retirement match at Revolution in March 2024. He survived a 2017 health collapse that put him in a medically induced coma, and as of August 2026, at 77, he is managing a blood clot in his leg that kept him from traveling to the Cauliflower Alley Club reunion — he accepted its Iron Mike Mazurki Award by video on August 26, with Jerry Lawler presenting, and reports himself otherwise well.

    05

    Factions

    He invented the modern wrestling stable, then joined its descendants.

    JCP/NWA & WCW · 1985–1999

    The Four Horsemen

    Ric Flair, Arn Anderson, Tully Blanchard, Ole Anderson, Barry Windham and later members

    The first modern stable and still the standard: four men in suits who justified every screwjob finish as excellence in numbers. Formed organically in 1985 out of the Andersons' kayfabe kinship with Flair, it protected his NWA reigns for a decade across multiple incarnations. Inducted into the WWE Hall of Fame as a unit in 2012 — Flair's second induction, making him the first two-time inductee.

    WWE · 2003–2005

    Evolution

    Triple H, Ric Flair, Batista, Randy Orton

    Past, present and two futures — Flair as the living credential beside Triple H's title reigns while Orton and Batista apprenticed. His late-career renaissance as a worker and talker ran through it, and both apprentices credit him as the education.

    TNA · 2010–2011

    Fortune

    Ric Flair, AJ Styles, Kazarian, Beer Money and others

    A deliberate Horsemen homage he fronted during his TNA run — the faction gathering TNA's best pure wrestlers under his name. The run ended his full-time wrestling; his last TNA match came in 2011.

    06

    Personas

    One character, worn for half a century and adjusted by decade: the Nature Boy name was taken from Buddy Rogers, the act made permanent after the 1975 plane crash forced the rebuild.

    NBMid-Atlantic · 1974–1981

    The Nature Boy, inherited

    Adopted Buddy Rogers' moniker and settled the claim against Rogers himself in a 1979 program. The robes, the strut and the bleached hair date from here; the crash-enforced style change — from brawling heavyweight to cardio machine — is what made the act tourable.

    CHNWA · 1981–1991

    The Touring Champion

    The limousine-riding, jet-flying, kiss-stealing, wheelin'-dealin' son of a gun — the custom-fit heel who defended the ten pounds of gold an hour a night in every territory the NWA had. The 'dirtiest player in the game' toolkit — the eye poke, the knee, the trunks, Woooo! — was standardized here.

    RWWWF · 1991–1993

    The Real World's Champion

    Arrived carrying the actual big gold belt after WCW fired him without recovering it, and called himself the real champion on their television — a shoot wearing an angle's clothes. Won the 1992 Rumble; the 'Elizabeth photos' program with Randy Savage followed.

    NAWCW, WWE, TNA, AEW · 1993–present

    Naitch

    The elder statesman version: Horsemen reunions, Evolution, the 2008 WrestleMania farewell, the un-retirement, the 2022 Last Match, and the legends-role years since — plus a cultural afterlife ('Ric Flair Drip', the 30 for 30) bigger than most active careers.

    07

    Career Timeline

    Rice Lake, 1972, to Nashville, 2022 — with a broken back three years in.

    1972

    Debut under Verne Gagne

    Debuts December 10, 1972 in Rice Lake, Wisconsin, out of Gagne's notoriously brutal AWA camp.

    1975

    The plane crash

    Breaks his back in three places in the October 4 crash that killed the pilot and paralyzed Johnny Valentine. Told he will never wrestle again at 26; returns within months, rebuilt as a different kind of wrestler.

    1981

    First NWA World Championship

    Beats Dusty Rhodes in Kansas City on September 17, Lou Thesz refereeing. The decade of the touring champion begins.

    1983

    Starrcade is built around him

    Takes the title from Harley Race in the Starrcade '83 steel cage on November 24 — the flagship event exists to crown him.

    1985

    The Four Horsemen form

    With Arn and Ole Anderson and Tully Blanchard — the modern stable is invented around his title.

    1989

    The greatest year anyone has had

    The Steamboat trilogy (February 20, April 2, May 7) and the Funk I Quit match (November 15) in a single calendar year — the peak of the American in-ring canon.

    1992

    WWF Champion

    Wins the Royal Rumble on January 19 from the No. 3 spot for the vacant title, trades it with Randy Savage through the year.

    1994

    The torch passed at the Beach

    Loses the unification match to Hulk Hogan at Bash at the Beach on July 17 — Hogan's first WCW match.

    2008

    The WrestleMania farewell

    Loses the career-threatening match to Shawn Michaels at WrestleMania XXIV on March 30, the night after his first Hall of Fame induction. The retirement lasts until 2009.

    2012

    First two-time Hall of Famer

    Inducted again with the Four Horsemen, having wrestled his last TNA matches in 2011.

    2017

    The health collapse

    A medically induced coma and surgery, chronicled in ESPN's 30 for 30 'Nature Boy' that November. He survives it and keeps showing up.

    2022

    The Last Match

    July 31, Nashville, at 73: he and Andrade El Idolo beat Jeff Jarrett and Jay Lethal at the Starrcast event run as Jim Crockett Promotions. This retirement holds.

    2026

    Mazurki Award, by video

    At 77, accepts the Cauliflower Alley Club's Iron Mike Mazurki Award remotely on August 26 — a blood clot in his leg limits long travel — with Jerry Lawler presenting and Jimmy Hart on stage.

    08

    Rivalries

    Who he went to war with — a list that is really a history of two decades of main events.

    Ricky Steamboat

    The purest rivalry in American wrestling: perfect heel against perfect babyface, run in the Mid-Atlantic in the late 1970s and perfected in the 1989 trilogy — Chi-Town Rumble, the Clash VI two-of-three-falls, WrestleWar. All three are canon; the series has been the workrate benchmark for every generation since.

    Dusty Rhodes

    The bleached-blond aristocrat against the son of a plumber — the NWA's defining class war. Flair won his first world title against Dusty in 1981, and the Horsemen's on-screen breaking of Dusty's hand and leg gave the territory era its most famous angles, and wrestling the term &ldquo;Hard Times.&rdquo;

    Harley Race

    The champion he had to take it from twice — most famously in the Starrcade '83 cage on November 24, 1983, after Race put a $25,000 bounty on his head. The passing of the NWA torch, staged as such.

    Terry Funk

    Funk attacked him after WrestleWar 1989 and piledrove him through a table; the I Quit match at Clash IX on November 15, 1989 is the best-blood-feud payoff of its decade, and the rare Flair program built on hatred rather than the belt.

    Randy Savage

    The 1992 WWF program — Flair's fabricated claim of a past with Miss Elizabeth, doctored photos and all — put the title on Savage at WrestleMania VIII on April 5 and back on Flair at the September 1 Hershey taping. They ran it back across WCW's mid-1990s.

    Hulk Hogan

    The match the WWF sat on in 1992 finally happened in WCW: Hogan took Flair's title at Bash at the Beach on July 17, 1994, and the two traded main events through the Nitro era — the wars of the 1980s' two defining champions, fought a decade late.

    Shawn Michaels

    One match, but the one everyone remembers: WrestleMania XXIV, March 30, 2008, career on the line — 'I'm sorry, I love you,' superkick, and the most protected retirement the company has staged. That it did not stick is part of the Flair story too.

    Records & Feats

    The records, stated with the disputes attached — starting with the only number about him anyone argues over.

    ★

    16 recognized world championship reigns — and a real dispute above that

    WWE recognizes 16, commonly broken out as eight NWA, six WCW and two WWF reigns. Flair himself claims 21, which Wikipedia calls the most agreed-upon figure, and counts in the literature run to 25 — the gaps come from reigns the NWA later struck from its records and short changes overseas. This site publishes the recognized number and the dispute together, because that is the honest state of the sources.

    ★

    First two-time WWE Hall of Fame inductee

    2008 individually — the night before the WrestleMania XXIV farewell — and 2012 with the Four Horsemen.

    ★

    The 1992 Royal Rumble performance

    Entered No. 3, survived roughly an hour, and won the vacant WWF Championship on January 19, 1992 — the only time the Rumble itself has decided the company's top title, and still the performance every long Rumble run is measured against.

    ★

    The 1989 year

    The Steamboat trilogy (February 20, April 2, May 7) plus the Funk I Quit match (November 15) — four matches from one calendar year permanently lodged in the American canon, with the Steamboat series' five-star Observer ratings among the most republished in that publication's history.

    ★

    Came back from a broken back to a 50-year career

    The October 4, 1975 plane crash broke his back in three places at age 26; he returned within months. His last match came on July 31, 2022 — 49 years and seven months after his debut — and he won it, at 73.

    ★

    An Intercontinental Champion at 56

    Beat Carlito at Unforgiven on September 18, 2005 — among the oldest champions in the title's history, in the middle of the Evolution-era renaissance.

    ★

    Iron Mike Mazurki Award, 2026

    The Cauliflower Alley Club's highest honor, accepted by video on August 26, 2026 — a blood clot in his leg kept him from the Las Vegas reunion — with Jerry Lawler presenting.

    Two things are deliberately loose here. The world title count is presented as a range with a recognized floor, because that is what the sources support; any page telling you Flair is simply 'a 16-time champion' or simply 'a 21-time champion' is choosing a side without telling you. And no career win-loss total is published: the territory-era records to build one do not exist.

    Frequently Asked

    Is Ric Flair a 16-time or 21-time world champion?

    Both numbers are defensible, which is why this page carries the dispute rather than picking silently. WWE recognizes 16 reigns — commonly split as eight NWA, six WCW and two WWF. Flair himself claims 21, which Wikipedia describes as the most agreed-upon count, and some tallies reach 25 by including reigns the NWA later struck from its records and brief overseas changes. The recognized 16 is the floor every source accepts; everything above it depends on whose record book you trust.

    Is Ric Flair still alive, and what is his status in 2026?

    Yes — he is 77 and retired from the ring. As of late August 2026 he is managing a blood clot in his leg that he says prevents five- and six-hour trips; it kept him from traveling to the Cauliflower Alley Club reunion in Las Vegas, where he accepted the Iron Mike Mazurki Award by video on August 26, 2026, with Jerry Lawler presenting. He reports otherwise feeling well. He has held a legends-role deal with AEW since 2023 and seconded Sting’s retirement match at Revolution in March 2024.

    When was Ric Flair’s actual last match?

    July 31, 2022, at 73 — not WrestleMania XXIV. The 2008 Shawn Michaels match was staged and honored as the retirement, but he returned in TNA by 2009 and wrestled there into 2011. The real ending was Ric Flair's Last Match in Nashville, a Starrcast event run under the revived Jim Crockett Promotions banner, where he and Andrade El Idolo beat Jeff Jarrett and Jay Lethal — Flair winning with a figure-four after brass knuckles reached him from ringside.

    What happened in the 1975 plane crash?

    On October 4, 1975, a chartered plane carrying several Mid-Atlantic wrestlers crashed, killing the pilot and paralyzing Johnny Valentine. Flair, 26, broke his back in three places and was told he would never wrestle again. He was back in months — having remade himself from a 300-pound brawler into the conditioned, chop-and-figure-four stylist the rest of the career was built on. Every history of him treats the crash as the hinge.

    What are the essential Ric Flair matches?

    Start with 1989: the Ricky Steamboat trilogy — Chi-Town Rumble (February 20), the Clash VI best-of-three-falls (April 2) and WrestleWar (May 7) — then the Terry Funk I Quit match at Clash IX (November 15). Add the Starrcade '83 cage win over Harley Race, the 1992 Royal Rumble hour, and the WrestleMania XXIV farewell against Shawn Michaels for the arc of the whole career.
