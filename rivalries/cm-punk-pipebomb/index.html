    CM Punk's Pipebomb

    The worked-shoot promo that briefly made anything feel possible again.

    CM Punk's pipebomb is one of the most famous promos ever cut. With his contract expiring, CM Punk sat cross-legged on the stage and delivered a worked-shoot speech airing real grievances about WWE, John Cena and the company's treatment of talent. It set up a hometown Chicago title match at Money in the Bank — which Punk won, then "left" with the belt.

    Timeline

    1. Jun 27, 2011

      On Raw, with his deal set to expire, Punk sits on the stage and delivers the "pipebomb" — a reality-blurring monologue that electrifies the audience before his mic is cut.

    2. Summer 2011

      The angle catches fire; Punk positions himself as the truth-telling voice against the company standard-bearer Cena.

    3. Jul 17, 2011

      At Money in the Bank in his hometown of Chicago, a white-hot crowd roars Punk to victory over Cena for the WWE Championship.

    4. Immediately after

      Punk kisses McMahon goodbye and escapes through the crowd with the title, "leaving" the company as champion.

    5. Late 2011

      WWE can't fully sustain the momentum, but the moment reestablishes Punk as a genuine top star.

    The Arc

    The pipebomb worked because it felt dangerous — nobody was quite sure how much of it was scripted. Punk aired grievances that sounded exactly like the real complaints of an undervalued star: about being passed over, about Cena's grip on the top spot, about the company's politics. Delivered with the timing of a stand-up comic and the venom of a man with nothing to lose, it blurred the line between character and shoot in a way WWE's carefully managed PG era almost never allowed.

    The genius was the stakes: Punk's contract really was expiring, so the threat that he'd walk out with the title felt legitimate. When he beat Cena in front of a rabid Chicago crowd and vanished with the belt, it created a rare, intoxicating sense that anything could happen. WWE never quite recaptured the lightning — the follow-through was muddled — but for one summer, the pipebomb dragged the raw unpredictability of the Attitude Era into the modern age and made CM Punk feel like the most important man in wrestling.
